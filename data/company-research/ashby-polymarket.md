# Polymarket — research
Provider/key: ashby:polymarket | company_type: product

What they do: Polymarket operates the world's largest prediction market — a platform where users trade on the outcomes of real-world events across politics, economics, sports, culture and current affairs. In its own words: "Built as a peer-to-peer marketplace with no centralized 'house,' Polymarket aggregates diverse opinions into transparent, market-based probabilities that reflect collective expectations about the future." It runs a central limit order book (CLOB) with on-chain settlement, and is expanding into **perpetual futures** and a separate regulated US entity ("Polymarket US"). Markets settle on-chain, so the engineering spans an off-chain matching engine and on-chain transaction lifecycle.

How they describe themselves: Growth- and ambition-forward. From its own JDs: "We're growing fast, both in terms of volume ($21B traded in 2025) and adoption as an alternative news source. **Our ambition is to become a ubiquitous beacon of truth in global media and we need your help adding fuel to the fire.**" On the work itself the tone is engineering-serious: "The problems are deep, the stakes are real, and the work will be immediately visible in production"; "This is backend exchange engineering in the truest sense: low-latency order matching, high-throughput order state management, and the infrastructure that keeps an exchange operating correctly under load"; "**Ownership here is real and broad.**" No enumerated public values list found.

Size / stage / funding: Private, venture-backed, high-growth crypto/fintech. $21B in volume traded in 2025 by its own statement. Roughly 51 active job listings across the company, so headcount is scaling quickly from a small base. Not disclosed-profitable.

Locations / HQ: New York City. Third-party job-aggregator analysis of its listings puts roughly **94% of open roles in New York and 6% remote**.

Remote policy: **On-site NYC by default.** Its careers listings describe most engineering, design, product, marketing, finance, legal, markets, operations and talent roles as "Full time, on-site in New York." A small set of exceptions is posted remote — CX (Customer Support, Escalation Engineer), Dev Tools, Head of Payments, Head of People. Note that the three relevant reqs in the registry carry the location string "New York, Remote" / "Remote, New York", which reflects an Ashby location list containing both options rather than a remote-first policy; the company's own framing is on-site-in-New-York as the default.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **No, and not close.** Where remote is offered at all it is US-remote (aggregators list the openings as "United States Remote"). Nothing in the JD bodies or the careers listings mentions Canada, international hiring, work authorization outside the US, or timezone flexibility. Max is a Canadian citizen with no US work authorization who cannot relocate, so an on-site-NYC-default employer with US-only remote exceptions is not viable. Additionally, Polymarket restricted US users for years under a CFTC settlement and is only now standing up a regulated US entity — its employment and compliance posture is US-centric.

Engineering & tech: Genuinely deep, low-level backend systems work — among the most technically serious in this batch. The reqs cover: owning "the CLOB engine's critical path — profile matching and orderbook hot paths, map settlement-sensitive state transitions, and drive improvements to latency, throughput, and async engine flow backed by benchmarks and production-grade evidence"; "reduce lock contention without breaking correctness… debug concurrency issues"; reasoning about "how on-chain transaction lifecycle, confirmations, reorgs, balances, approvals, and execution outcomes affect off-chain exchange state"; and building the matching engine, position management and order state for perpetual futures. The Senior Infrastructure Engineer, Platform req is a true platform role: "designing, building, and maintaining the backbone of our engineering platform: from developer workflows and CI/CD pipelines to core infrastructure supporting the high-performance APIs, data services, and real-time trading systems," with "the autonomy to make big technical decisions, drive system architecture, and implement solutions that balance speed, reliability, and long-term maintainability." A Senior C/C++ role exists for the US Exchange, indicating latency-critical native code.

Compensation: **None disclosed.** All three relevant Ashby reqs carry empty compensation objects — no tier summary, no salary components — and no pay-transparency ranges appear in the JD bodies. NY State pay-transparency law would normally require ranges on NYC-located postings; their absence here is itself notable. No company-level pay-philosophy page was found.

Notable / other: Polymarket is a crypto-native company operating in a heavily regulated and politically salient domain; it previously settled with the CFTC and blocked US traders, and is now building a regulated US offering. Its own copy positions it as a media/truth institution as much as a marketplace. Roles are described with high-ownership, high-visibility framing throughout.

Open relevant roles (sample): Senior Infrastructure Engineer, Platform (New York, Remote); Senior Backend Engineer, Exchange Infrastructure (Remote, New York); Senior Backend Engineer, Perpetuals (New York, Remote); Senior Backend Engineer, Onchain; Senior Backend Engineer, API; Senior Data Engineer; Software Engineer, Dev Tools; Software Engineer, Polymarket US.
