# GiveDirectly — research
Provider/key: greenhouse:givedirectly | company_type: product

What they do: Nonprofit that delivers unconditional cash transfers directly to people living in poverty, with no strings attached, on the evidence that recipients spend better than programs choose for them. Operates as a platform through which governments, foundations and individuals fund direct cash. Founded 2008; has delivered more than $500M to over 1 million families. Programs run in the DRC, Kenya, Malawi, Mozambique, Rwanda, Uganda and the United States.

How they describe themselves: Eight stated values, verbatim from their own values page (givedirectly.org/givedirectly-values) —
- **"Recipients first"** — "We prioritize recipient preferences over those of donors or ourselves."
- **"Team next"** — "We do what's best for organizational – not individual – success."
- **"Be proactively candid"** — "We say what we believe, and are honest in sharing information."
- **"Create positive energy"** — "We strive to be a source – not drain – of energy for our colleagues."
- **"Think rigorously; act quickly"** — "We are intellectually rigorous with a drive towards action – not debate."
- **"Know yourself and grow"** — "We recognize and accept our imperfections with a focus on growth."
- **"Accept reality. Propose solutions"** — "We do not dwell on problems. We work actively to create solutions."
- **"Be productively ambitious"** — "We take the risks to pursue industry-changing success, not incremental progress."
Marketing-side framing also uses "Rethinking Aid", "Impact-Driven" and "Global Team".
Job-posting tone describes the org as "lean, fast-moving, and high-autonomy" and explicitly invokes a "startup mentality" inside a mission-driven nonprofit. Stated hiring emphasis is on "emotional intelligence, problem solving, project management, follow-through, and fostering inclusivity."

Size / stage / funding: 800+ team members across 30 countries. Registered nonprofit — philanthropically funded (foundations, governments, individual donors) rather than venture-backed; no runway/funding-round dynamics, but income is grant/donation-dependent rather than commercial revenue. No profitability concept applies.

Locations / HQ: Headquartered in New York. Field operations in DRC, Kenya, Malawi, Mozambique, Rwanda, Uganda and the US. Nairobi used as a team retreat location.

Remote policy: Central support roles (including engineering) are "fully remote first." Beyond a required overlap window, their careers page states "how and when you work is entirely up to you." Home-office setup and desk allowance provided for eligible positions. The stated overlap requirement is at least three hours with East Africa Time (EAT, UTC+3); the Senior Software Engineer JD softens this to "available for East Africa timezone meetings, 2+ hours, a couple times weekly." Travel to operational countries roughly 1–2 times per year.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Unclear.** The Senior Software Engineer JD names the US, UK and Kenya explicitly with published salary benchmarks, and says other countries are "considered" with an estimated salary benchmark supplied during hiring. Canada is not named. Their general policy is that employees must already hold legal authorization to work in their chosen country — a Canadian citizen in Canada satisfies that, but Canada is not a confirmed employing geography. Timezone note: EAT is UTC+3 vs Mountain Time UTC−7, a 10-hour gap; an EAT-afternoon meeting lands in the early MT morning.

Engineering & tech: Small in-house engineering org; currently hiring a Director of Engineering and a Senior Software Engineer. Three engineering teams named in the JD: Programs Engineering, Fundraising Engineering, and International Rapid Response. Stack signals: backend work across "databases, cloud services, APIs"; strongly-typed OOP languages (Java, C#, Go) plus scripting (Python, Ruby); Python + AWS in production called out as a bonus, as is payments/financial software and microservice/serverless architecture. No public engineering blog or handbook surfaced.

Compensation: Publicly stated philosophy is to pay "generously and equitably," benchmarked competitively against peer organizations using an accredited third-party salary aggregator, with an explicit **no-negotiation policy**. Pay is set by role, location and cost of living. The Senior Software Engineer JD publishes concrete benchmarks: US $157,500 base + ~15% bonus + $21,393 benefits stipend (~$181,125 total); UK £108,150 base + ~15% bonus + £2,760 stipend; Kenya $91,500 base + ~15% bonus + $8,453 stipend. Benefits include top-tier healthcare, paid parental leave and statutory retirement.

Culture survey (their own careers page, 2026): 90% report strong belonging, 80% report access to meaningful growth opportunities, 83% feel the values guide daily decisions. Staff quotes cite a "healthy disregard for 'we've always done it this way.'" Their own words on hours: "Life doesn't run on a 9-to-5 schedule, and neither do we." No visa sponsorship for central support roles unless role-specific — you must already hold work authorization where you live.

Notable / other: Team spans 23–30 countries depending on the source. Unusually transparent about pay for a nonprofit — publishing per-country base/bonus/stipend numbers in the JD itself is rare. The organization is a well-known fixture in the effective-altruism / evidence-based-aid space.

Open relevant roles (sample): Senior Software Engineer (Remote); Director of Engineering (Remote).
