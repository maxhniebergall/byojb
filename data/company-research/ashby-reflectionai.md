# Reflection — research
Provider/key: ashby:reflectionai | company_type: product
What they do: Reflection (Reflection AI) is a frontier AI research lab building open-weight foundation models. Its own words: "Reflection is a research lab making intelligence open and accessible for everyone to use, customize, and build on. We build open models that let anyone control their intelligence and help shape the future of AI." Site tagline: "Build the future of open intelligence."
How they describe themselves: Mission — "make intelligence open and accessible to all". The JDs stress a "talent-dense team", "building from the ground up", and doing "the most impactful work of your career". Explicit cultural asks in the JD: "Thrive in a high-agency, fast-paced environment; bias toward action and impact" and "Excited about zero to one challenges — turning prototypes into real systems rather than polishing existing ones." Offer framing: "Top-tier compensation: Salary and equity structured to recognize and retain our talent globally", stock options for everyone, health & wellness benefits.
Size / stage / funding: Not disclosed on the fetched page and not verified here. It is a well-funded, high-profile frontier lab in aggressive hiring mode (22 live relevant openings, all "Member of Technical Staff"), i.e. an early, rapidly scaling organization rather than an established one.
Locations / HQ: Postings list New York, NY; San Francisco, CA; and London. No other geos appear.
Remote policy: No remote policy is stated on the site or in the JDs; every posting is anchored to the NY / SF / London trio. The only "hybrid" mention in the JD text refers to a "hybrid research–engineering role" (a role blend, not a work-location policy) — do not misread it as a location policy.
Remote-Canada eligibility: NO evidence of eligibility, and the location lists are city-anchored to NY/SF/London. Frontier labs at this stage are typically in-office by design. Treat as effectively disqualifying for a Kimberley, BC candidate; not formally contradicted anywhere, but nothing supports it.
