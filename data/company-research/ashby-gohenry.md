# GoHenry — research
Provider/key: ashby:gohenry | company_type: product

What they do: GoHenry is a children's money app and prepaid debit card with parental oversight — in their own words, "the money app giving kids a head start in life as they learn to earn, save, spend and invest – all with parental oversight." It launched in 2012 and states it "pioneered a new category in financial education"; over 2.3 million kids have used it. The stated ultimate goal is "to make every kid smart with money," on the belief that "the best way to learn is to get hands-on experience with money." Product features referenced include "Money Missions" (financial-education lessons) alongside the card and app.

How they describe themselves: Mission-driven financial literacy for children, with an explicit social-impact framing ("empower the next generation", "Tech for Good"). They highlight charitable impact: "GoHenry kids have donated over £770,000 of their own money to NSPCC via their GoHenry accounts." The engineering framing in the JD is unusually calm and craft-oriented rather than hustle-flavoured — emphasis on "blameless post-incident reviews", "a consistently high bar for scalability, resilience, and reliability", infrastructure that is "version-controlled, reviewable, and reproducible", "actionable alerting that surfaces real signals", and "A passion for sharing knowledge, mentoring peers, writing documentation, and helping fellow engineers grow." No "fast-paced"/"wear many hats"/"rocketship" language appears anywhere in the posting. Equal-opportunity statement included.

Size / stage / funding: Private; founded 2012, so well past the earliest stage. Headcount, funding stage and profitability are NOT stated on the posting and were not verified (web search exhausted this session) — unverified. Note for future re-use: GoHenry is known to have combined with a US children's-finance company, but that was not verified from a first-party source here and is not asserted.

Locations / HQ: United Kingdom. Inferred with high confidence from their own benefits list, which is entirely UK-specific: a "workplace pension plan", "8 UK bank holidays", Bupa Health Cash Plan or Bupa Private Medical, "Salary Sacrifice options", and "death in service" cover. Remote-Canada eligibility: NO — effectively disqualifying. Engineering & tech: Google Cloud shop — deep GCP including GKE and Cloud SQL, GCP networking and security model, Cloudflare as a bonus. Kubernetes in production, Terraform and Helm for infrastructure-as-code, modern CI/CD pipelines. Observability via Datadog (metrics, structured logging, actionable alerting for on-call). Security and compliance are first-class: PCI-DSS and SOC 2 audit support. Org structure: a dedicated Platform team that other "tech squads" consume capabilities from, partnering with backend, data and security engineers — i.e. an internal-platform model with internal customers, not customer-facing engineering.
