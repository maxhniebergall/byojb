# Buildout — research
Provider/key: ashby:buildout | company_type: product

What they do: Buildout is a vertical SaaS company serving commercial real estate (CRE) brokerages. In their own words: "Buildout is the AI deal engine for CRE brokerages, automating every step from first contact to commission. While brokers focus on relationships and winning listings, Buildout handles the workflows behind the scenes, turning manual processes into intelligent, scalable systems. Trusted by over 50,000 brokers, Buildout powers more profitable deals from lead to close." Their site describes the same product as "the AI brokerage solution that takes your team from contact to commission in one connected platform" — an integrated CRM, marketing automation, data management and back-office suite with "a single login and data layer to eliminate re-entry across tools." Named customers on their website include Coldwell Banker Commercial, Lincoln Commercial, NAI Global, Newmark, Lee & Associates and SVN — i.e. established national CRE brands rather than startups.

How they describe themselves: Product-quality oriented, with an in-house shorthand. "We create exceptional, easy to use, high quality products that lead the commercial real estate industry into the future and delight our customers." They repeatedly invoke "Buildout Quality" as a named internal standard, maintained by "performing thorough and timely code reviews, pairing on difficult issues to share knowledge and drive accountability, and delivering excellent software on a timely basis to lead by example." Their marketing positioning is efficiency-focused: "Less waste. Less friction. More profitable deals. More human work where it matters," and "AI that executes; not just assists." On culture, the careers text offers "A wonderfully quirky culture where you're encouraged to bring your whole self to work" and "Challenging problems to solve with a committed and supportive team who are invested in your growth and development." No formal values list was located.

Size / stage / funding: **Not stated and not verified.** Neither the job posting nor the company homepage discloses headcount, funding stage, ownership or profitability. Serving 50,000+ brokers across national brokerage brands implies an established, revenue-generating business rather than an early startup, and the org has an "SVP of Engineering" (Alan Spadoni, named as the reporting manager), which implies a multi-layer engineering organization. Beyond that, treat stage and financial position as unknown.

Locations / HQ: **Not stated on the pages available.** US-based on the evidence of 401(k), EEOC filings, Illinois-specific "order of protection status" in the EEO clause, and a California-employee privacy notice. The Illinois reference is a weak hint at a Chicago-area origin, but this is an inference, not a verified fact.

Remote policy: **Fully remote, and it is described as a culture rather than an accommodation.** Their own words: "A fully remote work culture with a monthly remote work reimbursement ($600/year) to support our distributed team and an annual, in-person company kickoff." The Ashby posting location field reads simply "Remote." So: distributed by default, with one annual in-person gathering. Async practices are not explicitly described, though the development process is structured (see below).

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Unclear, but probably not — no positive evidence, and several US-only indicators.** The posting says "fully remote" without ever stating a geographic restriction, which distinguishes it from Aescape, Bubble and Centralize in this batch, all of which say "US" explicitly. However, the surrounding benefits and legal text are uniformly US-specific: "401(k) with 4% company match and immediate vesting," two US medical plans, HSA seed, company-paid STD/LTD, EEOC identity questions ("It is required by law that we ask these questions using the format provided by the EEOC"), "14 paid company holidays," an Illinois-specific EEO clause, and a "Privacy Notice for Prospective California Employees." There is no mention of Canadian employment, an employer of record, or any non-US entity. On balance the absence of an explicit "US only" line is more likely an omission than an opening. **Do not treat as Canada-eligible without asking.**

Engineering & tech: A Ruby on Rails shop with a React frontend. Their words: "Ruby/Rails: We're a Ruby shop at Buildout, so deep knowledge of Rails will help you get up and running quickly. Our app uses all of the standard Rails goodness you know and love, as well as additional tools for design, business logic, and background jobs." Frontend: "Our front-end is built on React for all new work, alongside a mature codebase with legacy JavaScript we're actively modernizing." Testing is emphasised: "We're investing in our test suite (RSpec and Jest, with growing Playwright end-to-end coverage) and providing ample team resources to make sure that everything we create is tested and rock-solid." Other stated tech: "git for source control, Slim for our view templates, lightly customized Bootstrap for our CSS, MySQL, and AWS."

Process: teams pair an engineer with a Product Manager and UX Designer, and work runs through "our milestone-based feature development framework, helping your team lead break large projects into well-scoped, estimable components so work starts with a clear plan and multiple engineers can contribute in parallel."

**AI-native development is a defining and unusually explicit part of their engineering identity.** Verbatim: "Engineering at Buildout is AI-accelerated by default. Claude Code is our primary development agent with Claude and Cubic reviewing every PR. We expect AI to be embedded across your implementation, debugging, testing, and documentation work as repeatable workflows, not occasional autocomplete." They define four criteria — Delegation ("You know what to hand to an agent, what demands your own hands, and when AI is the wrong tool entirely"), Description, Discernment ("You catch subtly wrong code, missing edge cases, and security issues in AI output"), and Diligence ("You own everything you ship. You can delegate the work, but not the accountability"). The interview reflects it: "you'll use AI tooling on a realistic problem, and we're watching how you prompt, validate, and push back."

Notable / other: The posting sets explicit ramp expectations — "90 days: You're shipping production PRs within your first few weeks... 6 months: You own feature-level work end to end and you're contributing meaningfully to our automated test coverage. 12 months: You're trusted to scope and estimate large projects." Benefits, in their words: two medical plans, "100% coverage of employee dental and vision insurance premiums, HSA seed, company-paid STD, LTD, life insurance, and telemedicine, and a wellness benefit of $400/year"; "Flexible PTO, 14 paid company holidays, paid parental leave, and give back days"; 401(k) with 4% match and immediate vesting; $600/year remote work reimbursement. Reporting line for the open role: Alan Spadoni, SVP of Engineering. The company is explicit that qualifications are flexible: they want "Multiple years of product software development experience within a SaaS company, or the ability to demonstrate you've learned enough in less time to still be a great engineer."
