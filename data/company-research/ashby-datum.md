# Datum — research
Provider/key: ashby:datum | company_type: product

What they do: Datum builds open-source cloud networking and control-plane infrastructure for "alt clouds" — smaller and specialist cloud providers. Their stated mission: "to help 1k clouds thrive in the AI era by unlocking internet superpowers for every builder. We're working in the open to bring the foundational capabilities that all the big guys use (private networking, peering, direct interconnection, etc) into the hands of builders and modern 'alt clouds' — no network team required." The core product is Datum Cloud, whose control plane is built on an open-source project they call **Milo**, described as "our open source business operating system… a toolkit for modern AI-forward alt-clouds."

How they describe themselves: Two values are named explicitly in their JDs. (1) **Connectors** — "One of Datum's core values is to be connectors: of applications, services, networks, and people. As such, this role (just like all roles at Datum) will work directly with users, customers, partners, and the broader community." (2) **Open by default** — "from how we license our code (AGPLv3) to how we communicate, engage with, and document our work. We believe this engenders trust from our customers and community, but also because open is the best way to build. It's more secure, more operable, and even more enjoyable." They close with: "We believe in openness, clarity, and collaboration. To learn more about how Datum aims to operate, please review our public handbook." A public handbook therefore exists, but its URL is not given in the JD and could not be located (web search unavailable this session) — unverified.

Engineering & tech: Stack, as listed by them: Languages Go and Rust; Data PostgreSQL, GraphQL, Elasticsearch, Meilisearch; Infrastructure Kubernetes, Flux, Pulumi (and Zitadel on the auth role); Cloud Cloudflare, AWS, GCP, Azure with multi-cloud networking; Monitoring Prometheus, Grafana, OpenTelemetry and network flow analysis; Development GitHub, CI/CD, automated testing, network simulation. Work areas: Kubernetes control-plane development (custom resources, controllers, kubebuilder), distributed systems design, consensus, async reconciliation, fault tolerance, multi-cloud and hybrid networking, CNI plugins, service mesh, edge/CDN architectures and global traffic management, with SRv6, eBPF, DPDK, VPP and mpTCP called out as pluses. The Auth role is federated authn/authz specifically: OAuth across multiple IdPs, SPIFFE and Workload Identity Federation, ABAC/RBAC/PBAC, and the Zanzibar family (SpiceDB, OpenFGA, Cedar) — "someone who wakes up every day thinking about federated authentication and authorization… can likely rattle off a few RFCs."

Notable / other: The role carries an explicit public/external dimension: contributing to public repos, engaging via GitHub issues and RFCs, "speaking at networking conferences and writing technical blog posts", mentoring external contributors, and representing Datum in technical working groups. Code is AGPLv3. Both roles state 5–6+ years of production Kubernetes at scale "with security as a first principle". No compensation is posted on either role.
