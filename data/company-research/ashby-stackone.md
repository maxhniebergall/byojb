# StackOne — research
Provider/key: ashby:stackone | company_type: product

What they do: In their own words: "StackOne is the integration infrastructure for AI agents. Backed by GV and Workday Ventures ($24M raised), we make it possible for any AI agent — whether our customers build it into their product or buy it off the shelf — to take real action across the enterprise stack. Our platform gives agents 430+ connectors and 26,000+ pre-built actions, an AI connector builder for everything not covered yet, and the production layer agents actually need: semantic tool discovery that cuts token use by up to 80%, managed authentication and per-action permissions, the most accurate prompt injection defense on the market, and end-to-end observability." They also describe themselves as "the tools gateway for agents: the secure, token-efficient layer through which AI agents reach 200+ enterprise SaaS systems." Customers are enterprises and AI-product builders; the product is B2B integration and governance middleware.

How they describe themselves: The recurring self-image is speed and trajectory — "Join us on our **fast trajectory** to build the future of agentic integrations", and a benefit listed as "Join one of **Europe's fastest-growing startups**". Team pedigree is a stated selling point: "Work with a veteran team of ex-Google, Microsoft, Oracle, Coinbase, JP Morgan and more." Engineering principles that are stated concretely and are more substantive than the marketing copy: "Treat infrastructure as a product: paved roads and self-serve tooling so product engineers ship without waiting on you"; "Templates and golden paths, not gatekeeping"; "A clear writer: docs and runbooks that internal engineers and external customers can follow"; "End-to-end ownership: you scope, ship, and measure, and automate instead of running manual checklists"; "Security-minded: you bake secure defaults into pipelines and repo templates, and partner with security rather than routing around it"; "Make the system easy to operate when something breaks at 3am." AI-assisted workflow is an explicit expectation: "Use AI in the workflow: lean on LLMs and agents for IaC generation, test scaffolding, and runbook drafting, with guardrails you trust" — including for "vibe-coded prototypes".

Size / stage / funding: Early-stage venture-backed startup. **$24M raised**, investors **GV (Google Ventures)** and **Workday Ventures**. Small enough that the Senior Platform Engineer "report[s] directly to the CTO" and works "closely with our Security Engineer" (singular) and tech leads. Exact headcount UNVERIFIED.

Locations / HQ: **London, United Kingdom.** Both live relevant postings list "London" as the location; benefits include a "£15/day London office lunch budget" and UK-specific schemes (EMI share options, Cycle2Work, Electric Cars scheme).

Remote policy: Stated verbatim at the end of the JD: "**London (hybrid, ~2 days/week) preferred; open to remote within the UK.**" So: hybrid-first in London, with fully-remote possible but bounded to the United Kingdom.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **No — verified negative, stated explicitly by the company.** "Open to remote within the UK" is an unambiguous country restriction, and compensation/benefits (EMI options, £ allowances, UK statutory-style holiday) are constructed entirely around UK employment. A Kimberley BC worker is also 8 hours behind London, which would make the hybrid-leaning, London-centred team structure unworkable even setting employment aside.

Engineering & tech: A well-documented, modern stack, published in the JD.
- Cloud & infra: AWS (ECS Fargate, Aurora Postgres, ElastiCache, MSK, OpenSearch, Lambda, S3, KMS, CloudFront, WAF), plus GCP and Cloudflare (Workers, WAF).
- IaC: Terraform and AWS CDK — actively "migrating off CDK".
- Data & messaging: Postgres, Redis, Kafka, OpenSearch, ClickHouse.
- CI/CD: GitHub Actions, consolidating toward a **monorepo** with incremental/affected-only test execution.
- Observability & analytics: Datadog, Sentry, Metabase.
- Languages: TypeScript (Node.js) and Python; Go acceptable. Docker and self-run Kubernetes.
Distinctive work: shipping into customers' own clouds (**BYOC / self-hosted**) via Terraform modules, container images and runbooks; a versioned, signed release and upgrade path with a supported-version policy and call-home usage reporting; SLOs and incident response. The AI Governance role is a separate specialisation — a policy/rules engine (authoring, evaluation, enforcement, versioning), provisioning lifecycle for tool access (enrol, grant, rotate, revoke), continuous posture and risk assessment of connected tools, telemetry and anomaly/abuse detection on the gateway hot path, and identity integration via OAuth 2.1, SSO and SCIM. They frame it explicitly by analogy: what MDM/UEM (Jamf, Kandji, Intune), EDR/XDR (CrowdStrike, SentinelOne) and API gateways (Kong, Apigee, Zuplo) did for devices and API traffic, applied to agent tool access.

Notable / other: Benefits are unusually well itemised — "Meaningful share options (EMI)"; "25 days holiday + 1 additional day per year of tenure"; private health insurance including dental and optical; £15/day London lunch budget up to £180/month; "£1,000 home office setup + £500/year top-up"; "Annual team offsite to sunny spots (last ones were in Croatia and Portugal)"; health/fitness/gift-card discounts; Cycle2Work and Electric Cars schemes. Neither posting states a salary range. Prior comparators they name for the BYOC problem: Nuon, Replicated, Omnistrate.

Open relevant roles (sample): Senior Platform Engineer (London); Senior Engineer (AI Governance) (London); AI Engineer.
