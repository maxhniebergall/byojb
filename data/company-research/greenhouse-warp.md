# Warp — research

Provider/key: greenhouse:warp | company_type: product

What they do: Warp builds developer tooling. It "began with the vision of reimagining one of the
fundamental dev tools — the terminal — to make it more usable and powerful for all developers," and
has since "evolved beyond its terminal roots into a full agentic development environment: a
workbench for dispatching agents to code, deploy, and debug production software." Two products
today: **Warp**, the agentic development environment grown out of the terminal, and **Oz**, "our
orchestration platform for running hundreds of cloud agents in parallel" — custom agents that are
"triggered programmatically, tracked centrally, and are shared across full teams… without
compromising security or reliability." Warp's built-in agent interoperates with third-party agents
including Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and OpenCode. The customer is the individual developer.

How they describe themselves: Mission — **"We build tools to make people powerful."** Careers
tagline — "The future doesn't build itself. Join us." Stated values from their JD: "We value
**honesty, humility, and pragmatism**, and our core product principle is **focusing on the user**."
They describe themselves as "a company run by product-first builders, building a core product for
all developers. We are committed to understanding our users deeply." Also "a community of curious
learners, and most of us are learning some skills for the first time (like our engineers learning
to program Warp in Rust)." Their stated mission has been stable through the AI pivot: "to empower
developers to ship better software more quickly, freeing them to focus on the creative and
rewarding aspects of their work." There is an internal "How We Work" guide referenced in JDs but it
is not publicly reachable (the obvious URL 404s).

Size / stage / funding: **Series B.** "$50M Series B funding round ($73M total raised), led by
Sequoia Capital" announced August 2023. Other investors: Google Ventures, Neo, and Box Group, plus
an angel roster including Dylan Field (Figma), Elad Gil, Jeff Weiner (LinkedIn), Marc Benioff
(Salesforce) and Sam Altman (OpenAI). Headcount not disclosed on their own pages. The early team
came from Google, Dropbox, Gem, LinkedIn and Facebook. Traction claims: "over 750k active
developers at companies including Docker, Ramp, Peloton, and over half of the Fortune 500 and
revenue that grew over 44x last year, Warp is one of the fastest-growing startups in the AI
development space."

Locations / HQ: San Francisco and New York offices.

Remote policy: **Remote-first, with optional offices.** Their careers page states it plainly:
"Remote + real offices: Remote-first across the US & Canada, with offices in SF & NY." The JD
repeats it: "A flexible remote-first culture, with optional office spaces in NYC and SF for folks
who want to work together IRL." They also run "twice-a-year all company retreats in awesome
locations."

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Verified yes, explicitly.** The careers page says "Remote-first across
the US & Canada," and the single relevant open role is posted with location "Remote in the US or
Canada." They additionally offer "sponsorship and ongoing support for international teammates." No
timezone requirement is stated in the JD, though the company's centre of gravity is SF/NY.

Engineering & tech: **Rust** is the primary language for the native app ("our engineers learning to
program Warp in Rust"), with listed bonus skills of "building native apps in Rust or WASM", "shell
or Linux-programming", "server-side programming in go", and "OpenGL, WebGL, or other graphics
programming" — so a native-client + graphics + Go-backend stack. The advertised Software Engineer
role is deliberately generalist and full-stack: "Participate in all aspects of software development
activities, including design, coding, code review, unit/integration testing, bug fixing, and
code/API documentation… Work across the entire technical stack to ensure users have the most
efficient and delightful Warp experience. The features you develop will span all aspects of the
user experience, making you an expert on their needs." Engineers "collaborate closely with the rest
of engineering and other stakeholders from our growth, marketing, and product teams to plan
features," and "prototype ideas and concepts to get quick feedback from internal and external
users." They run an optional public hiring challenge at
https://github.com/warpdotdev/hiring-challenge.

Notable / other: Benefits are strong and specific — 100% medical/dental/vision coverage for
employees (80% for dependents), 12 company holidays, 20 days PTO plus unlimited sick time, 16 weeks
paid parental leave for both birthing and non-birthing parents, pre-tax FSA/HSA and commuter
benefits, 401(k) through Vestwell, complimentary OneMedical membership, monthly gym and internet
stipends, home-office and professional-development stipends. Inclusive-application language:
"if you're excited about this role, but your past experience doesn't align perfectly with every
qualification… we encourage you to apply anyways!"

Compensation (posted, first-party): Warp publishes an explicit compensation-philosophy paragraph on
its JDs: "Warp operates from a place of high trust and transparency around compensation. We state
the pay range that best aligns with our understanding of the market and the needs for the open
role… We consider your skills, the level of experience you bring, and what similar jobs in the
market pay, all while ensuring equal pay for equal work at Warp." Total comp is stated as three
components: "(1) a market-competitive base salary, (2) a generous benefits package, and (3)
meaningful equity." For the open **Software Engineer** role: "The budgeted compensation amount for
this role is targeted at **$165,000 to $260,000**." Equity is "a meaningful stock option grant with
a four-year vesting period and one-year cliff," and they state plainly that "your equity is where
most of the significant upside potential is" — i.e. cash is deliberately not the whole story. They
offer to "walk you through different valuation scenarios at the offer stage."

Open relevant roles (sample): Software Engineer — "Remote in the US or Canada".
