# Sourcegraph — research
Provider/key: greenhouse:sourcegraph91 | company_type: product

What they do: Sourcegraph builds code intelligence infrastructure for large engineering organizations — code search and navigation across huge multi-repo codebases, Deep Search, an MCP server, and Agentic Batch Changes for executing coordinated code changes across hundreds of repositories at once. Their current positioning is explicitly the "context layer" for AI coding agents. Customers named on their own JDs include Stripe, Uber and Dropbox. Investors: a16z, Sequoia, Redpoint.

How they describe themselves: In their own words: "Our mission is to bring clarity and control to the world's most complex codebases. AI is accelerating code creation, but the infrastructure to understand, oversee, and evolve that code hasn't kept pace… As agentic development becomes the dominant engineering paradigm, we provide the context layer teams need to take control of their codebase." They describe themselves as "proud to operate as a globally distributed team that values high agency, direct communication, and customer love," and as "an open and transparent company." The JD closer is "If you want to build the infrastructure that lets every engineering team — and every agent they deploy — operate on their codebase with confidence, join us."

Size / stage / funding: Private, late-stage VC (a16z, Sequoia, Redpoint). Headcount is in the low hundreds after a significant contraction from its peak — the company went through layoffs and a strategic reset in the 2023-24 period, moved the main product to a non-open-source license, and re-focused hard on enterprise and AI/agent context. So: established product and blue-chip customers, but a company that has already demonstrated it will resize.

Locations / HQ: San Francisco on paper; in practice a globally distributed all-remote company with teammates across the US, Europe and elsewhere. No office attendance expectation.

Remote policy: All-remote and handbook-driven. Their public handbook (github.com/sourcegraph/handbook) documents compensation, leveling, benefits and process — a strong async/documentation-culture signal. Every JD carries an explicit "Hours & location" section rather than a vague policy. For the roles in scope: "While we hire almost anywhere in the world, we have a preference for someone to reside in the following locations… However, if you feel qualified, we welcome you to apply regardless of location. No matter what, working hours must overlap with EST for at least 20 hours/week." Preferred locations for the Platform [IC3] role: EST and Europe.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Yes, with a timezone caveat.** Postings are location-typed simply "Remote" and the company states it hires almost anywhere in the world; Canada is a routine hiring geography for them and is covered by their four-zone international pay model. The binding constraint is not geography but hours: 20 hours/week of overlap with EST. From Mountain Time (EST−2) that is comfortably achievable on a normal workday. The soft preference for EST/Europe means Mountain Time is later than ideal but not disqualifying. Employment outside the US is arranged through their "How we engage talent outside of the US" process (EOR/entity-dependent) — worth confirming whether a Canadian hire is employee or contractor.

Engineering & tech: The Platform team builds Sourcegraph Cloud, the managed SaaS offering: inter-cloud connectivity for regulated enterprise customers, a control plane orchestrating a fleet of single-tenant Cloud instances, exposing complex infrastructure as APIs for internal customers, and diving into upstream dependencies (CDK for Terraform, GCP Terraform providers) to debug and patch non-trivial problems. Also advocacy for reliable services and operational excellence, with an on-call rotation upholding contractual SLAs. Other tracks include Code Understanding and Code Plane (source retrieval, storage, indexing and serving across multiple code hosts). Predominantly Go, with heavy GCP, Terraform and Kubernetes exposure. JDs include structured 1-month / 3-month / 6-month ramp expectations — unusually explicit scoping.

Notable / other: Compensation is unusually transparent for a private company. Per the public handbook, band entry points are benchmarked to the **75th percentile of US technology market data**, the top of a band is **15% above the entry point**, bands are reset every six months alongside the Impact Review Process, and both US and international locations fall into one of four cost-of-labor zones with the company stating it targets the high end "to ensure we're always paying above market regardless of where you live in the world." Zone starting salaries are printed directly on the JDs (see Open roles below).

Open relevant roles (sample): Software Engineer - Platform [IC3] — Remote (IC3 band starts: Zone 2 $148,000, Zone 3 $111,000, Zone 4 $74,000 USD); Software Engineer/Tech Lead - Code Plane [IC5] — Remote (IC5 band starts: Zone 1 $240,000, Zone 2 $192,000, Zone 3 $144,000, Zone 4 $96,000 USD); Software Engineer - Code Understanding [IC2] — Remote (IC2 band starts: Zone 1 $160,000, Zone 2 $128,000, Zone 3 $96,000, Zone 4 $64,000 USD).
