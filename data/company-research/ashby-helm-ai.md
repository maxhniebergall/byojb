# Helm.ai — research
Provider/key: ashby:helm-ai | company_type: product
What they do: Helm.ai builds AI software for autonomous driving and robotics, spanning Level 2 ADAS through Level 4 autonomy. Its differentiator is a proprietary "Deep Teaching" methodology — unsupervised learning that "can train neural networks without the need for human annotation or simulation" and is hardware-agnostic, which they describe as "uniquely data and capital efficient". Products: Helm.ai Vision (perception for Level 3), Helm.ai Driver (vision-only autonomy stack), GenSim-3 (generative scenario validation), VidGen-3 (synthetic driving video generation) and WorldGen-1 (multi-sensor simulation foundation model). Customers/partners: Honda Motor and Volkswagen, on "production-bound projects", plus "some of the world's largest automotive manufacturers".
How they describe themselves (their own words): "We help make autonomous technologies more efficient, safer, and accessible." Team framing: "Our team is made up of people with a diverse set of experiences in software and academia. We work together towards one common goal: to integrate the software you'll help us build into hundreds of millions of vehicles." On pace, stated repeatedly and without hedging: "expect a fast-paced environment; you will have significant influence over the company's business and the pace of technical innovation"; "We need people who are organized, able to think on their feet, and can find creative solutions to challenging problems"; and for the research roles, "Proven ability to thrive in fast-paced environment" and "A sense of practical optimism: not all experiments are successful".
Size / stage / funding: Private venture-backed; "we've raised over $100M from Honda, Goodyear Ventures, Mando, and others to help us scale". Headcount not disclosed. Automotive-OEM revenue with long design-in cycles; strategic investors are also customers.
Locations / HQ: Silicon Valley (their words), with a South Bay office referenced in benefits ("Free lunch and fully-stocked kitchen in our South Bay office").
Remote policy: Distributed hiring by geography-tagged req rather than a stated policy. Live reqs are tagged "Remote - US" (five of six) and "Remote - Canada" (one). Benefits copy is US-centric — "401K plan management", US-style health insurance, South Bay office perks — with no Canadian benefits described.

Engineering & tech: Python is the stated core language for the ML roles. The Machine Learning Engineer role is research operations rather than platform work: "collaborate with researchers to perform research operations using existing infrastructure", characterize "neural network quality, failure modes, and edge cases", manage "a large number of concurrent experiments", review results and write technical reports for external parties; computer vision and deep learning experience is a plus, with a Master's/PhD or 5+ years listed as preferred not required. The Staff Software Engineer role — US-only — is the genuine infrastructure job: "Architect and design scalable, reliable, and efficient ML infrastructure and services", large-scale training and massive datasets, platform cost/flexibility optimization, end-to-end ownership from inception to deployment and monitoring, plus mentorship.
Notable / other: Pay is stated on the Machine Learning Engineer JD: "The pay range for this position is estimated to fall in the base range of approximately $150,000 and $250,000", varying by location and qualifications, with possible equity and bonus. The currency is not labelled and the identical text appears on both the US and Canada variants of the req, so the CAD/USD question is genuinely ambiguous — recorded as USD with that caveat. Benefits: competitive health insurance, 401K, free lunch and stocked kitchen in the South Bay office, monthly wellness stipend, office setup allowance, company retreats.
