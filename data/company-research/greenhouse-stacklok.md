# Stacklok — research
Provider/key: greenhouse:stacklok | company_type: product

What they do: Stacklok builds **enterprise infrastructure for AI agents**. Its thesis is that
enterprises are moving agents from experimentation into production and need to run, govern and
secure them behind their own firewall. The core product is the **Stacklok Enterprise Platform**, the
production distribution of **ToolHive** — its Apache-2.0 open-source platform for running and
governing **Model Context Protocol (MCP)** servers across Kubernetes and private-cloud environments,
so agents can reach internal data and systems while meeting security, compliance and operational
requirements of regulated organisations. It has extended this to the model layer with an
**enterprise AI gateway** (including LLM spend control). Stacklok also helps maintain the official
MCP registry and contributes to the community shaping the protocol; ToolHive ships support for the
2026-07-28 MCP spec. The platform is described as already adopted by leading technology companies
and organisations in regulated industries, with Fortune 500 / Global 2000 customers signing on now.

How they describe themselves: mission is to "ensure all knowledge workers can simply and safely lean
into AI agents to increase their productivity". Culture language from their own pages and JDs: "we
believe great technology is built by teams that support, challenge, and inspire one another"; "we
are **AI maximalists**, confident in its potential and committed to ensuring it is used in ways that
are safe and sustainable"; "a highly motivated, collaborative team with deep experience building
some of the world's most impactful technologies, with strong roots in open source, cloud-native
technologies, security, and developer tools"; and about the founders, "the leadership team likes and
trusts each other, and that sets a tone for the company". They invite people who "care deeply about
their craft, their community, and each other".

Size / stage / funding: founded **2023** by Craig McLuckie and Luke Hinds. Backed by **Accel,
Madrona and Bain Capital**. Headcount is not published but this is clearly an early-stage startup
(single-digit or low-double-digit engineering team implied by the JD talking about mentoring "SWE
and SWE II engineers"). Early revenue — customers are described as signing on now and the platform
as still "being hardened for their production environments".

Leadership: **Craig McLuckie** (Founder/CEO) and **Joe Beda** (CTO) are two of the co-creators of
Kubernetes at Google; both previously co-founded Heptio, which VMware acquired. Luke Hinds is a
cloud-native security veteran (Sigstore). COO Shanis Windland, CMO Scott Buchanan. This is an
unusually credentialed team for the stage.

Locations / HQ: global/distributed, no meaningful office requirement stated; regular in-person team
offsites "in unique destinations".

Remote policy: the company page says "hybrid or fully remote roles depending on team/location", and
the JDs offer "a flexible, hybrid-or-remote work environment". Benefits: competitive compensation
and equity, comprehensive medical/dental/vision, flexible PTO and paid holidays, paid parental
leave, and the offsites.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Unclear, and geographically awkward.** Both live engineering
requisitions are posted as "**Remote: North America - East**", and the Senior JD states in a
dedicated Location section: "This is a remote role based in North America, with a **strong
preference for candidates located in the U.S. Eastern Time Zone**." Neither posting names Canada
explicitly, and neither mentions Canadian employment. "North America" nominally includes Canada and
Eastern-Canadian candidates would clearly qualify, but nothing confirms a Canadian payroll entity,
and a Kimberley BC (Mountain Time) candidate sits **two hours behind** the stated preferred zone —
the opposite direction from what they want. This must be confirmed with a recruiter before investing
in an application.

Engineering & tech: Kubernetes and private-cloud infrastructure, MCP server runtime and governance,
an AI/LLM gateway, and open-source development in the ToolHive codebase alongside community
contributors. The Senior role's scope is genuinely infrastructural and well-defined: "own subsystems
within Stacklok Enterprise", design and deliver whole subsystems end to end from technical design
through production, anchor projects and make the design trade-offs that determine how the subsystem
evolves, write production-ready code with observability/testing/error handling built in, produce
design docs and work breakdowns, mentor SWE and SWE II engineers and strengthen code-review culture,
and contribute upstream to open-source ToolHive. Success criteria at 6-12 months include shipping a
complete subsystem to production for enterprise customers and becoming the go-to owner of a platform
area. They also explicitly measure "how AI-assisted workflows helped and where they created
rework" — AI-assisted development is part of the expected workflow. Hiring language is inclusive:
"We do not expect every candidate to meet every point below."

Compensation: **no salary range is stated on either live posting**, and no public pay-transparency or
compensation-philosophy page was found. The JDs say only "competitive compensation and equity".

Notable / other: the pitch leans hard on timing — "the timing is what makes it compelling. Customers
are signing on now, the platform is being hardened for their production environments, and you will
solve hard infrastructure problems in an emerging category alongside a team whose founders
co-created Kubernetes." Public assets include the ToolHive repo (github.com/stacklok/toolhive), the
Stacklok blog (which publishes substantive technical writing on agentic-system risk and tool
annotations), and the official MCP registry work.

Open relevant roles (sample): Sr. Software Engineer - Platform (Remote: North America - East);
Software Engineer II - Platform (Remote: North America - East).
