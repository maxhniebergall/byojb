# Nylas — research
Provider/key: ashby:nylas | company_type: product
What they do: Nylas sells communications-infrastructure APIs. In their own words: "At Nylas, we specialize in making it easier for developers to add email, calendar, and contact management features into their applications. We provide APIs, which streamline the integration of these functionalities, ensuring they are secure and effective." The platform lets developers "integrate with Gmail, Microsoft, IMAP, Zoom, and 250+ mail, calendar and meeting providers in 5 minutes." Product surface: Email, Calendar, Scheduler, Notetaker (meeting intelligence), Transactional Send, Inbound Email, Domains, Logs, and a newer **Agent Accounts** product that gives "your AI agent its own email and calendar." Named customers include Dialpad, Clio, Dayforce, TalentReef, FounderSuite and RecruiterPM.
How they describe themselves: "We are building the infrastructure that powers modern communications. Our APIs and platform help developers integrate email, calendar, scheduling, contacts, notetakers, and agent accounts into their applications without managing the complexity underneath." Also: "Supporting over 100,000 developers and collaborating with more than 900 companies globally, Nylas plays a pivotal role in how digital communication tools are built and utilized… By reducing the barriers in communication technology, we empower developers to innovate and enhance user interaction across platforms." Marketing themes are "enterprise reliability", "seamless authentication", and being "the infrastructure that powers communications". No formal published values list was found; the JD's cultural asks are "A bias toward shipping. You move quickly, iterate based on feedback, and hold a high bar for quality without getting stuck in long planning cycles" and "Comfort working in ambiguous product areas."
Size / stage / funding: Venture-backed private company. Scale self-reported as "1000+ companies" trusted, 100,000+ developers, "34.5B+ API transactions and 200TB of data processed daily." Headcount, funding stage and profitability are **not disclosed** on the site and were not verified here. Historically a Series-C-stage developer-infrastructure company (unverified in this pass).
Locations / HQ: Not stated on the site page fetched. The live engineering posting is listed as "Remote – Toronto, Ontario", indicating a Canadian (Ontario) hiring presence. San Francisco HQ is the historical understanding but is **unverified**.

If you are not located in or able to work from a state/province where Nylas is registered, you will not be eligible for employment. Visa sponsorship may not be available in certain remote locations."

The only province evidenced is Ontario. There is no evidence Nylas is registered as an employer in British Columbia, and the posting's own Toronto anchoring suggests Eastern-Time alignment. This is unverified rather than confirmed negative; a candidate in Kimberley would have to ask directly.
Engineering & tech: The open role centres on the **Nylas Dashboard** — "the control plane for that platform… where customers create applications, connect accounts, configure domains, manage grants, debug issues, explore product capabilities, monitor logs, and understand how their integrations are performing." Scope is explicitly full-stack: "You will work across the stack, owning the user-facing surfaces, APIs, services, data models, product workflows, and internal platform patterns." Primary stack named: React, TypeScript, component-driven architecture. Nice-to-haves reveal the wider platform: Next.js, GraphQL, REST APIs, **Go, gRPC, Postgres, Redis**, cloud-native service architectures, plus observability, logging, billing, authentication and authorization surfaces. Strong AI-native framing throughout — "Build AI-native and agent-facing experiences", "Experience using AI tools in your own development workflow", agent-powered products and LLM-powered developer tools. Bar: "4+ years of experience as a software engineer building user-facing products."
Notable / other: Interview process is published — Round 1 a 30-minute hiring-manager intro; Round 2 a 60-minute live technical assessment; Round 3 "The Loop", a series of 45-minute team chats capped at 3 hours, where "we look for the 'what, why, and how' behind your best work." Compensation is deliberately unstated: "Actual compensation will be determined based on individual qualifications, which are objectively assessed during the interview process."
