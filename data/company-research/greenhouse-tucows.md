# Tucows — research
Provider/key: greenhouse:tucows | company_type: product

What they do: Tucows (NASDAQ:TCX, TSX:TC) is a Canadian internet-infrastructure holding company. In
their own words: "Tucows is possibly the biggest Internet company you've never heard of. We started as
a simple shareware site in 1993 and have since grown into a stable of businesses: Tucows Domains, Ting
Internet and Wavelo." Also: "As OpenSRS, Enom, Ascio and Hover, we're the second largest domain
registrar in the world. As Ting, we're investing to build fiber-optic Internet infrastructure across the
US." Wavelo is their telecom/MVNO software platform. They call themselves "an overnight success story
30 years in the making."

How they describe themselves: "We're a team of smart, skilled people united in our belief of the power
of the open Internet" and "proof positive that good people doing good work nets good results." On
culture: "We embrace a people-first philosophy that is rooted in respect, trust, and flexibility. We
believe that whatever works for our employees is what works best for us." The careers page describes a
"people-first culture... built on trust, inclusivity, and transparency" and a workplace "where every
Herd member feels supported, respected, and empowered", with flexibility for "caregiving, health,
accessibility, or future planning." They also disclose their own AI-in-hiring stance: they use "AI to
support our hiring process, but never at the expense of authenticity," with "all final decisions made by
real people." Employees are called "the Herd" ("join the herd!").

Size / stage / funding: Publicly traded (NASDAQ:TCX / TSX:TC), founded 1993, 30+ years old — not
venture-runway dependent. "Today, over one thousand people work in over 20 countries to help us make
the Internet better." Note the Ting fiber build is capital-intensive and Tucows has carried debt to fund
it; the domains business is the cash engine. (Financial detail beyond the self-description was not
fetched this session.)

Locations / HQ: Toronto, Ontario, Canada. Distributed across 20+ countries.

Remote policy: Remote-first, stated plainly by them: "the majority of our roles are remote-first,
meaning you can work from anywhere you can connect to the Internet!" and, on the careers page, "a
remote-first company, with most job opportunities offering remote work options." They run a Nomad
program letting employees "work abroad for up to 90 days per year." The Data Platform Engineer JD's
own extracted signals list "people-first philosophy rooted in respect, trust, and flexibility" and
"majority of roles are remote-first, work from anywhere."

Remote-Canada eligibility: VERIFIED YES. The live "Data Platform Engineer" posting is listed with
location "Canada" (country-wide, not city-scoped) and is remote-first per the company's stated policy.
A Canadian-HQ public company hiring country-wide is about as clean a Canada-remote signal as exists.
No Mountain-Time-specific statement was found, but the JD does state a willingness "to participate in
on-call and after-hours" work.

Engineering & tech: The live Data Platform Engineer JD is a detailed, first-party picture: AWS, GCP and
OpenStack; Terraform; Docker/Kubernetes/EKS/Helm; Snowflake, BigQuery, dbt, Airflow (MWAA), Kafka,
DataHub, Fivetran, Stitch, legacy Pentaho; Prometheus, Grafana, CloudWatch; Looker; GitHub; RBAC/IAM;
Python and bash. Notably they have built internal "Claude Code-based tooling, including agents, skills,
hooks, and MCP integrations" and want the hire to "help shape how the team uses AI to improve
engineering workflows." Work framing is explicitly platform/reliability, not feature delivery: "Keep the
data platform healthy... Make infrastructure changes safely... Improve automation and tooling... Support
access control and governance... Document how things work: Write clear runbooks, platform documentation,
and operational guides so knowledge is easier to share and the team is less dependent on any one
person." They are mid-migration: "Help move workloads from our legacy stack to the modern data platform,
including validation, cutover, and decommissioning work." Honest about ramp: "There will be a lot to
learn here, even if you arrive experienced. That's the appeal, not the catch."

Notable / other: Only one relevant role is live right now, so volume is thin. The role is pitched at
3+ years, i.e. mid-senior, which caps both scope and pay. The domain (domains registry, fiber, telecom
billing software) is precisely the "boring, mission-critical utility" category.

Pay (first-party): the live Data Platform Engineer JD posts CAD $90,700 - $113,400 base.

Open relevant roles (sample): Data Platform Engineer (Canada, remote, Analytics & Insights);
Intermediate Software Engineer - Artificial Intelligence (AI).
