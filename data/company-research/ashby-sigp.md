# Sigma Prime — research
Provider/key: ashby:sigp | company_type: product (see classification note below)

What they do: Sigma Prime works on Ethereum core infrastructure and blockchain security. Their words: "Sigma Prime is a blockchain security and distributed systems consultancy focused on securing critical decentralised infrastructure. Since 2016, we have worked with many of the most prominent teams in the industry, delivering security assessments, protocol research, design consultancy, and specialist engineering services across complex Web3 systems, distributed protocols, zero-knowledge systems, infrastructure, and smart contracts." Their flagship engineering output is a product, not a service: "We are also the creators and maintainers of Lighthouse, an Ethereum consensus client used by roughly 40% of the network."

Classification note: the company self-describes with the word "consultancy", but it is not a staffing/body-shop or outsourcing firm — it maintains one of the most widely-deployed pieces of open-source infrastructure on the Ethereum network, and the advertised role is internal infrastructure work supporting that engineering, not client placement. It is recorded as `product` rather than excluded as `consulting` on that basis; a reviewer who considers the audit/assessment services line dominant may disagree, and the ambiguity is flagged deliberately.

How they describe themselves: Low-ceremony, high-autonomy, and refreshingly plain. Verbatim under "WORKING STYLE": "We provide high-level strategic guidance and constructive feedback, but we do not micro-manage. Candidates must be comfortable owning their day-to-day work, identifying problems, proposing improvements, and driving tasks to completion without waiting for detailed instructions. We are especially interested in people who notice fragile or repetitive processes and turn them into reliable automated systems." And, verbatim, the entire stated cultural bar: "No jerks or bullies." Language expectation is explicitly humane: "Candidates must be proficient in technical conversations in English, both written and verbal. Perfect English is not required."

Engineering & tech: Purely infrastructure, with no product/frontend dilution. Scope: "build, automate, operate, and improve infrastructure for Ethereum network development, testing, and production systems... supporting everything from local test environments to high-performance production systems. This role is centred around reliability, automation, operational ownership, and long-term infrastructure design." Concretely required or preferred: strong Kubernetes and "Terraform/OpenTofu"; administering self-hosted clusters ("RKE2, K3s, or bare-metal Kubernetes"); Kubernetes internals (deployments, ingress, storage, upgrades, rollbacks, failure recovery); Kubernetes networking (service discovery, DNS, load balancing, cross-cluster connectivity); multi-provider Kubernetes; cost optimisation "for cost-efficient, high-throughput blockchain workloads"; CI/CD for containerised applications; strong Linux/server administration, "shell scripting, systemd, journald"; server security, patching, access control and hardening; networking fundamentals (routing, DNS, domain management); "Ansible, inventories, roles, Jinja2 templates, and YAML". Also large-scale network testing and testnet tooling: "Our work requires both small-scale and large-scale network testing under a variety of real-world conditions. You will create tooling, automation, observability systems, testnets, and deployment infrastructure."

Blockchain requirement: Domain knowledge is wanted but not gating — "Candidates with extensive DevOps experience but limited blockchain experience will still be considered. However, enthusiasm for Ethereum and decentralised systems is essential." Expected concepts: blocks, forks, chain state, peer discovery, and a high-level grasp of signature schemes such as ECDSA.

Notable / other: Lighthouse's ~40% consensus-client share means the infrastructure here is genuinely load-bearing for a public network — about as close to "utility" as software gets. Counterweight: crypto as a sector carries market-cycle employment risk, and the JD posts no compensation.
