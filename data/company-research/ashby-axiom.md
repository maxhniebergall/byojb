# Axiom — research
Provider/key: ashby:axiom | company_type: product

What they do: In their own words, "Axiom is using zero-knowledge proofs to enable new types of verifiable and private software applications, starting with crypto and fintech." Their flagship release is OpenVM: "a performant and modular zkVM framework built for customization and extensibility. OpenVM allows developers to verify the correct execution of arbitrary computer programs in a flexible and performant way." Their business is two-sided — "We are building ZK-enabled products using OpenVM and supporting teams building on OpenVM" — so they are both a product company and an infrastructure/framework maintainer for other crypto teams. The open role concerns "Rust-based rollup nodes and sequencers to deliver zero-knowledge proof based products on-chain."

How they describe themselves: "We are a small but incredibly strong team backed by the best investors in crypto. We're looking for exceptional people who are excited to work on challenging and impactful problems in a fast-paced environment." The stated hiring bar is heavily talent-density-oriented rather than values-oriented — there is no values or principles list anywhere in the material. What stands in for values is the "Who you are" list: "You are extremely detail oriented and have a security mindset"; "You're a clear communicator and value collaboration"; "You have a bias towards action and extremely strong work ethic." They also emphasise pragmatism: "We also value the ability to make pragmatic technology choices which empower our small team."

Size / stage / funding: Small, private, venture-backed. They say only "backed by the best investors in crypto" without naming them; no round, headcount or valuation is stated, and none was verified. "Small but incredibly strong team" and "empower our small team" are the only size indicators.

Locations / HQ: New York City. "Our office is in NYC and working in person in NY is our strong preference, but we're open to remote candidates as well."

Remote policy: **Office-first with reluctant remote.** The company's own framing is a strong in-person preference in New York, with remote treated as an accepted exception rather than a default. The Ashby location field reads "New York, Remote," consistent with that. No async practices, handbook, or distributed-team infrastructure is described — and for a company that says in-person is the preference, the absence is meaningful.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Unclear — not stated either way, and I could not verify it.** The JD says "we're open to remote candidates as well" without qualifying the geography — unlike several peers in this batch, it does not say "US-based" or "must have US work authorization." That silence is genuinely ambiguous rather than encouraging: no Canadian entity, employer-of-record arrangement, or non-US hiring is mentioned anywhere. The posted salary is USD and the office is NYC. For a small NYC crypto startup that would prefer you at a desk in Manhattan, a Canada-based remote hire is possible but should be treated as unestablished. Compounding this: even if employment were possible, the company has told you that remote is the less-preferred path, and Mountain Time is three hours behind an office-centric NYC team.

Engineering & tech: Rust-first. "You are fluent in Rust, C++, or a similarly performant backend language. We work primarily in Rust." Also required: production cloud operations with "orchestration and containerization (Docker, Terraform, Kubernetes)," experience "deploying and monitoring high uptime applications," and "experience working within a large production codebase." Bonuses: "experience working with blockchain nodes like Geth, Reth, or similar," "low-level details of Ethereum," and "experience working in an adversarial or security critical environment." The substance of the role is distributed systems — rollup nodes and sequencers — with a hard security and correctness emphasis: "designing secure and efficient systems and making design choices to enable rapid development without sacrificing security."

Notable / other: The founding and team pedigree is unusually academic and is given at length. "Our founders Yi Sun and Jonathan Wang met while studying math at Harvard and have been working on ZK since 2021. Together, they wrote some of the most performant ZK circuits for elliptic curve cryptography and Ethereum data reads before starting Axiom." Yi Sun "was a professor at UChicago Statistics and did a math PhD at MIT," previously "an advisor for Gauntlet and worked at an HFT startup from inception to profitable trading." Jonathan Wang "published number theory research in top math journals and did a math PhD at UChicago." The wider team is described as "alums of Applied Intuition, Coinbase, Color, Neon, Google, and Perplexity as well as math / CS PhDs and IMO / IPhO / Codeforces winners."
