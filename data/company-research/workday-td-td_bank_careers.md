# TD Bank Group — research
Provider/key: workday:td/td_bank_careers | company_type: product (in-house enterprise technology, not a vendor)

**Entity confirmation:** JD bodies carry TD Bank Group boilerplate ("TD is committed to providing fair and equitable compensation opportunities to all colleagues", "Line of Business: Technology Solutions", "TD Securities") and Toronto work locations. Confirmed as the Canadian bank, not a name collision.

What they do: TD Bank Group is one of Canada's Big Five chartered banks and a top-10 North American bank, with retail and commercial banking in Canada and the US, wealth management, and TD Securities (capital markets). The engineering roles in the registry are in-house enterprise technology: core banking platforms, reconciliations, AML/transaction-monitoring (OFSAA), Salesforce/Loyalty Cloud, data platforms on Azure Databricks, capital-markets Java/Kafka systems in TD Securities, and mainframe-adjacent work (one live posting explicitly requires COBOL, JCL, VSAM, TSO, ISPF, IMS DC/DB).

How they describe themselves: The JD boilerplate is corporate-HR rather than mission prose: "TD is committed to providing fair and equitable compensation opportunities to all colleagues. Growth opportunities and skill development are defining features of the colleague experience at TD. Our compensation policies and practices have been designed to allow colleagues to progress through the salary range over time as they progress in their role." Employees are consistently called "colleagues." Role descriptions are written in formal architecture language — e.g. "Lead the creation of comprehensive end-to-end detailed solutions for projects and platforms with overall accountability for the technical design and delivery of a specific technical solution based on requirements from stakeholders… ensuring adherence to engineering and architectural design principles while meeting business requirements… with a focus on efficiency, reliability, scalability, and security… in compliance with enterprise and industry standards." Notably free of hustle vocabulary; the tone is procedural and stakeholder-driven.

Size / stage / funding: Publicly listed (TSX/NYSE: TD), roughly 95,000 employees group-wide, one of the largest and most stable employers in Canada. Zero runway risk. Recent years have included a significant US AML enforcement settlement and an associated remediation program — which is visibly reflected in the hiring (AML/transaction-monitoring platform roles are live in the registry).

Locations / HQ: HQ Toronto, Ontario (TD Centre; one posting names 661 University Avenue). Essentially every live engineering posting in the registry is Toronto, Ontario; a couple are "2 Locations" (Toronto plus a US site) and one Data Engineer III is US-based.

Remote policy: TD operates a hybrid model with in-office expectations tied to a named work location. The postings state "Work Location: Toronto, Ontario, Canada" and "Hours: 37.5"; the extractor reads `remote_policy: unclear` on those with bodies, with `timezone: America/Toronto` throughout. No fully-remote engineering posting appears anywhere in the 29 postings on record. No public statement of a remote-work policy was fetched.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **No, in the sense that matters here.** These are Canada-eligible jobs but Toronto-anchored ones: every relevant posting names a Toronto work location with a Toronto timezone, and none is advertised as remote. A Toronto hybrid/in-office role is not workable from Kimberley, BC without relocating. Treat `geo_eligibility: canada` on these rows as "Canadian legal entity", not "remote anywhere in Canada".

Engineering & tech: Large in-house enterprise stack. Observed across live postings: Java/Spring and Kafka (TD Securities capital markets), Azure Databricks for data engineering, Salesforce/Loyalty Cloud, OFSAA for AML transaction monitoring, mainframe COBOL/JCL/VSAM/IMS, and a small amount of AI-integration work ("Software Engineer II/III (AI Integration)", "Machine Learning Engineer", "Senior Machine Learning Engineer"). Engineering is organised as a "Technology Solutions" line of business supporting internal stakeholders, with a formal, ladder-based levelling scheme (Associate → Software Engineer I → II → III → Senior → Staff) that maps cleanly onto pay bands.

Compensation: Unusually well-documented, because TD posts pay ranges on its Ontario JDs. Observed directly from live JDs, all CAD base: Software Engineer I / Data Engineer I ≈ $69,700–$98,400; Software Engineer II ≈ $81,600–$115,200; Software Engineer III and Software Engineer III (AI Integration) $125,500–$154,000 (flagged as reflecting "a temporary market premium specific to this role that is reassessed annually"); Data Platform Engineer II $126,800–$156,000; Senior Java Engineer, TD Securities $126,800–$164,100; Machine Learning Engineer II $154,000–$199,500; Machine Learning Engineer (senior) $120,000–$250,000. Eleven band rows are already on record in `data/company-comp.jsonl`. Equity is explicitly absent on the TD Securities rows.

Notable / other: A genuinely stable, pension-and-benefits employer with formal levelling and public pay transparency — the opposite of startup risk. The trade-off is enterprise process weight, stakeholder-driven scope, and a heavy legacy surface (COBOL/mainframe roles are actively posted). The ML pay bands are the most competitive thing here; the general software bands are below market for senior infra work.

Open relevant roles (sample): Lead Platform Engineer, TD Securities; Senior Java Engineer, TD Securities (Java, Kafka); Staff Software Engineer; Software Engineer III; Engineer II – Senior Data Engineer (Azure Databricks); Senior Machine Learning Engineer; Data Engineer III; Software Engineer I - AI.
