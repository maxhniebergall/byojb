# The Cigna Group — research
Provider/key: workday:cigna/cignacareers | company_type: product

What they do: The Cigna Group (NYSE: CI) is a global health company. Its Cigna Healthcare arm is a health-benefits provider offering medical, dental, vision, pharmacy and behavioural-health insurance through two divisions — U.S. Employer (over 15 million medical customers) and International Health (~17 million customers across 200+ countries). Its Evernorth arm is the health-services side, which includes Express Scripts, one of the largest pharmacy-benefit managers (PBM) in North America; Express Scripts Canada "is the leader in health benefits management. Serving over 12 million members, we help insurance carriers, third party administrators, and the public sector optimize the value of health benefits". "HIH" in the job titles is their India delivery centre branding (Evernorth Health Innovation Hub, Hyderabad).

How they describe themselves: Cigna Healthcare describes itself as "an advocate for better health through every stage of life", aiming to empower customers "with the information and insight they need to make the best choices for improving their health and vitality", with a whole-person focus across behavioural health and wellness. The Canadian JD states values plainly: "a positive work environment built on solid corporate values, integrity, mutual respect, collaboration, passion, service and alignment", plus strong accessibility commitments — "We are committed to creating a working environment that is barrier-free and we are prepared to provide accommodation for people with disabilities." Tone is corporate and measured; no hustle or hype language anywhere in the captured JD text.

Size / stage / funding: Public (NYSE: CI). Cigna Healthcare reports total revenues of $274.9 billion as of December 31 2025 and approximately 67,700 colleagues, with 185+ million customer relationships and ~1.7 million provider relationships. Very large, very stable, not funding-dependent.

Locations / HQ: US operations centred on Bloomfield CT and Philadelphia PA. The overwhelming majority of engineering postings are in Hyderabad, India. Other geos in current postings: Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia, and Mississauga, Ontario (Express Scripts Canada).

Remote policy: Office-anchored. The one Canadian posting states its arrangement explicitly: "Work Arrangement: Hybrid". No remote-first or async signals appear anywhere in the captured JDs.

Remote-Canada eligibility: NO. Of 28 live relevant postings, 27 are located in Hyderabad, India (or US/Malaysia sites) and are not open to a Canada-based worker. The single Canadian posting — "Machine Learning Engineer - Express Scripts Canada" — is explicitly hybrid in Mississauga, Ontario, i.e. Eastern time with regular office presence in the Greater Toronto Area. From Kimberley, BC that is a relocation, not a remote role. There is no remote-anywhere-in-Canada option on this board.

Engineering & tech: The Canadian ML role sits in Express Scripts Canada's Application Delivery Department and covers "data pipelines and ML services that detect unusual patterns in healthcare claims and pharmacies" — "hands-on data engineering (Oracle + ETL), model development (unsupervised learning), and API serving (Python/FastAPI)", including feature ingestion from Oracle via SQLAlchemy/oracledb, argument-driven CLI tooling, logging and automation. Elsewhere the org hires heavily into a banded internal ladder (Associate Advisor → Senior Analyst → Lead Analyst → Advisor → Senior Advisor → Associate Principal → Principal) across software engineering and infrastructure engineering, including GRC/compliance infrastructure and IBM i (AS/400) engineering — a real mainframe/legacy estate alongside modern work.

Notable / other: The Canadian posting states its range openly ("Pay Range: $115,000 - $125,000 annually", CAD, with the caveat that "this is a general posting range and offer range will be varied based on relevant experience, qualifications, skills required and location"), and notes the vacancy is a replacement. Background/clearance documentation may be required. Job titles use analyst/advisor nomenclature rather than engineer levels, which makes seniority hard to read from titles alone.
