# Common Room — research
Provider/key: ashby:commonroom | company_type: product

**Material status change: Common Room is being acquired by Zoom, announced 2 July 2026.** Anything below about independent strategy, hiring and roadmap should be read in that light.

What they do: In their own words, "Common Room is the AI GTM Platform that empowers your teams with AI agents built on complete buyer intelligence. We unify every signal across the full buyer journey into one continuously-updated, person-level view—so your team knows exactly who to target and why, what to say, and when to reach out." Concretely: it ingests buying signals from across many sources, does identity resolution to a person-level profile, and drives automated sales outreach and GTM agents. It started life (2020-21) as a community-intelligence platform for developer/community-led growth and has since repositioned around AI sales intelligence. Target customers are GTM orgs of 75-1000+ seats.

How they describe themselves: Their pitch: "GTM teams are drowning in tool sprawl, fragmented data, and AI that doesn't deliver. Common Room replaces your disconnected stack with one platform built for scale and complexity." Their four stated values, verbatim from their own JD:
- **Be Customer-centric** — "We work backwards from the needs of our customers. The crisp articulation of customer value guides our decisions."
- **Strive for Simplicity** — "We choose simplicity over complexity whenever possible. We seek to identify and understand the essential quality of what we are building."
- **Make it Happen** — "We are quick to take the first step, and prioritize decisiveness over fear of making a mistake. We don't confuse motion for movement and we measure ourselves on impact over actions."
- **We're In this Together** — "We measure personal success by the success of our customers and teammates. Relationships matter, and the strongest ones are built on the foundations of trust, enablement, and transparency."
Engineering-culture language in the JD is notably grounded: they want someone who has "good judgement on tradeoffs and tools… and doesn't over index on trendy/fashionable tech. You generally prefer supported, stable technologies," who considers "observability, SLA and scalability implications, but [is] able to avoid premature optimization," and who "builds sustainably, and [is] capable of managing your time and priorities as best suits your needs." Countervailing signal from the same JD: "you also identify and participate in important company-building initiatives that a fast growing startup needs."

Size / stage / funding: Founded 2020, emerged from stealth 2021. Raised over $52M from Index Ventures, Madrona Venture Group, Greylock, Next Play Ventures, 01 Advisors, plus angels including Etsy CEO Josh Silverman and former Twitter CEO Dick Costolo, and 25+ operators from Figma, Stripe, Airtable, Slack, Notion and Loom. Headcount roughly 180-183. Being acquired by Zoom as of the 2 July 2026 announcement.

Locations / HQ: Seattle, Washington; distributed team with remote roles across the US and Canada.

Remote policy: Remote-friendly with location-typed requisitions and a stated work-from-home policy including laptop and home-office support. Benefits listed on their own JD: competitive base plus "meaningful equity ownership," medical/dental/vision with 100% of employee premium and 50% of dependent premiums paid, HSA/FSA, unlimited PTO and paid company holidays.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Verified yes for one role.** Their open engineering reqs are typed "Remote - Canada" (Senior Software Engineer, Backend), "Remote - US" (Software Engineer, Backend) and "Remote (US)" (Backend Software Engineer – Multiple Openings). The senior backend role is explicitly country-level Canada remote with no metro attached, so a BC resident is eligible. Seattle HQ means Pacific Time anchoring, which is the friendliest timezone in this batch for Mountain Time. The caveat is durability, not eligibility: post-acquisition, Zoom's own hiring geographies and req approvals will govern, and these postings may not survive integration.

Engineering & tech: Stated stack, verbatim: "we prefer to primarily use TypeScript with Node.js. We use TypeGraphQL for our API endpoints, Apollo/Express as our web server, and Postgres (hosted on RDS) + ClickHouse as our database. We use React for front-end, and AWS for our infrastructure needs." The senior backend role is described as building "APIs that other engineers can use to solve customer problems," ensuring "systems are efficient, scalable and reliable," and keeping "core internal capabilities, infrastructure, and systems… one step ahead of the product." They ask for 6+ years, independent ownership of large backend projects, collaboration with application/front-end engineers, and comfort with "AWS, Postgres, and Node.js." ClickHouse plus person-level identity resolution across many signal sources implies genuine data-pipeline and scale work underneath the GTM product.

Notable / other: The published values and JD are unusually candid and anti-hype for a startup — explicit preference for stable technology over fashionable technology, and explicit endorsement of sustainable pace and self-managed priorities. That sits oddly alongside "fast growing startup" company-building expectations. The Ashby ATS records no posted compensation on any of the three reqs (all compensation fields are null).
