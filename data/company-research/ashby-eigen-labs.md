# Eigen Labs — research
Provider/key: ashby:eigen-labs | company_type: product

What they do: Eigen Labs describes itself as "a frontier lab building coordination technologies for
the post-AGI world." Concretely, it builds **open networks across several layers of the AI stack:
agentic research, inference infrastructure, and capital formation**. It is the company behind the
EigenLayer / EigenCloud ecosystem (crypto-economic restaking and verifiable-compute infrastructure on
Ethereum) — the JD's closing "nice to have" line, "Interest in crypto, distributed systems, or
verifiable compute," is the giveaway, alongside "non-cash compensation (tokens and equity)." A named
sub-effort, **Darkbloom**, is "already building distributed AI infrastructure from idle hardware
around the world." They also cite a recent result in which "a community of scientists, amateurs, and
agents can collaborate to surpass Google's unpublished quantum breakthrough, showcasing the power of
open coordination."

How they describe themselves: Heavily ideological, and worth quoting in full because it is the
company's own framing. Thesis: "AGI is completely reconfiguring power, and a few major labs are
already consolidating this power. We believe that coordination through open networks is the only
counterforce that lets individuals maximize their agency." And: "A small number of companies are
rapidly becoming gatekeepers to the most powerful systems ever created, trained on open internet, open
research, and open-source software, now locked behind permission structures and centralized control.
The question isn't whether AI will transform society. The question is who gets to participate."
Their stated beliefs, verbatim:
- "Scientific discovery should remain open."
- "Intelligence should not be controlled by a handful of institutions."
- "Individuals should be able to coordinate, build, and create without asking for permission."
- "AI should empower people, not make them dependent."
- "AI should expand individual agency, not concentrate power."
Working style is stated bluntly: "You will own large parts of the system and move quickly from idea →
working product. You will spend most of your time building. Not managing. Not talking about roadmaps.
Building." Hiring philosophy: "We care more about evidence than years of experience. Show us you've
built hard things that work," with an explicit signal being "You move fast and iterate - you don't
wait for perfect specs."

Size / stage / funding: Private, venture-backed, Seattle-based. Compensation includes **tokens and
equity**, so it is a crypto-token company as well as an equity one. **Exact headcount, funding round
and runway are unverified** — no first-party page was fetched (the only registry URL is an Ashby board,
which is client-rendered and not fetchable, and the session's web-search budget was exhausted). The
self-description "frontier lab" and the breadth of parallel initiatives suggest a well-capitalised but
early and fast-moving organisation; treat that as inference, not fact.

Locations / HQ: **Seattle**, per the JD's "close collaboration with the Seattle-based team." No other
offices confirmed.

Remote policy: "This is a full-time role, **remote-friendly, with a preference for U.S. time zones**
and close collaboration with the Seattle-based team." Benefits list "Flexible hours and a supportive
remote environment," a remote work set-up stipend, flexible time off, and yearly off-sites. Note the
wording is "remote-friendly," not remote-first, and it is anchored to a physical Seattle team.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Plausible but unverified — not confirmed.** Two signals point toward a
path: the benefits section says "If hired outside of the US through an **EOR**, benefits will be
offered based on specific country requirements and EOR offerings," which means non-US hiring does
happen; and the timezone preference is US time zones, which Mountain Time (Seattle is Pacific, one

Canada is nowhere named, the benefits block is titled "US Benefits," and the stated salary range is

Engineering & tech: The Senior Agentic AI Engineer role is squarely agent-infrastructure work: "Build
agent runtimes and orchestration systems (planning, tool use, memory, coordination)"; "Make agents
reliable (retries, failure handling, state management)"; "Make agents observable (tracing, debugging,
evaluation)"; "Make agents cheap (cost-aware execution, performance optimization)"; "Make agents
useful in production (not demos - real systems people depend on)"; "Integrate LLMs, APIs, and external
data into coherent, working systems"; "Define how developers build, debug, and extend agent behavior."
Required foundation: strong backend/systems experience (distributed systems, APIs, infrastructure),
proficiency in Python, Go, TypeScript or Rust, reliability patterns (state, retries, observability),
and "comfort working across the stack when needed." Nice-to-haves name agent frameworks (LangChain,
AutoGen, CrewAI or custom), multi-agent orchestration, LLM evaluation and performance tuning, workflow
engines (**Temporal, Airflow, Dagster**), developer platforms/SDKs/internal tools, and retrieval
systems, vector databases and memory architectures.

Compensation (objective): The JD states a target salary range of **$187,000 – $253,000 USD**,
"determined by a few factors including your skillset, prior relevant experience, quality of interviews
and market factors at the point in time of offer," plus "short- and long-term incentives" and
"non-cash compensation (tokens and equity)." US benefits: medical/dental/vision, 401(k) with company
match, monthly wellness benefit, paid parental leave, flexible time off, remote setup stipend, yearly
off-sites. A direct band row for ml_eng/senior from this exact JD **already exists** in
