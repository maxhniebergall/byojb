# Instrumentl — research
Provider/key: lever:Instrumentl | company_type: product

What they do: Instrumentl is a grant-management SaaS platform for nonprofits — grant discovery, application writing, tracking and award management in one system. In their own words: "Nonprofits do some of the most important work in the world, and most of them are still managing grants in spreadsheets. We're fixing that." They describe the product as "the operating system for grant-funded organizations," connecting "discovery, applications, and award management." Public numbers from their site: 5,500+ active customers, "$1.1B+ in grants managed," a database of 360 million opportunities, 450,000+ funder profiles and 36,000+ active RFPs.

How they describe themselves: The JD boilerplate is the fullest statement of identity: "Instrumentl is a profitable, hypergrowth, YC-backed SaaS platform building the operating system for grant-funded organizations. More than 5,500 nonprofits use Instrumentl to discover, track, and win grant funding, from local community organizations to the San Diego Zoo and the University of Alaska. Collectively they've moved over $1 billion through our platform. We're growing more than 40% year over year, customers love us (Ellis PMF 60+), and we're hiring people who want to build something that matters." Their site emphasises building "for and with grant teams" and a support model staffed by "former grant professionals." No separate published values list was found. Note the two-sided self-description: "profitable" (a stability signal) sitting directly alongside "hypergrowth" and "more than 40% year over year."

Size / stage / funding: Private, Y Combinator-backed, and self-described as **profitable** — an unusual and meaningful claim for a company of this stage, and the main stability signal here. Growth stated at 40%+ YoY. Headcount not published; the customer base and the small number of open engineering roles suggest a company in the tens-to-low-hundreds of employees.

Locations / HQ: Not published on the site (unverified). Operates as a distributed company based on how roles are posted.

Remote policy: Remote. All three live engineering roles are posted as "Remote - USA / Remote - Canada" with no office location named at all, which is how a fully distributed employer tags roles.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Yes — stated on every live engineering role.** All three postings (Senior Backend Engineer, Senior Data Engineer, Sr. Software Engineer GenAI) carry "Remote - Canada" alongside "Remote - USA." Canada is a first-class hiring geography, not an exception. No province restriction is named and no timezone requirement appears in the posting metadata, so BC/Mountain Time should be workable — worth confirming, since a US-heavy company can still expect Eastern or Pacific overlap.

Engineering & tech: Not well documented — the JD bodies captured in the registry contain only the company boilerplate, with the role-specific responsibilities and stack not present in the fetched text. What can be said from the role titles is that they run a three-way split of backend, data engineering and generative-AI engineering, all at senior level, which for a company this size implies real data-pipeline substance (a 360-million-opportunity grant database with 450,000+ funder profiles is a serious ingestion, matching and search problem). Stack unverified.

Notable / other: No salary range is posted on any of the three live roles, despite both being remote-US and remote-Canada (so no transparency jurisdiction is forcing disclosure). The domain — moving grant money to nonprofits — is a mission-positive, non-adversarial one. "Ellis PMF 60+" refers to their product-market-fit survey score.
