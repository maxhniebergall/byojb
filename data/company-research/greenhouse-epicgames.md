# Epic Games — research
Provider/key: greenhouse:epicgames | company_type: product

What they do: "Epic Games is the creator of Fortnite, Unreal Engine, and the Epic Games Store — a company that has shaped the way billions of people play, create, and connect. We build foundational technology that powers some of the largest interactive experiences on the planet, and we give those tools to creators and developers around the world through open ecosystems." Revenue comes from Fortnite and its ecosystem, Unreal Engine licensing, and the Epic Games Store.

How they describe themselves: "At the core of Epic's success are talented, passionate people. Epic prides itself on creating a collaborative, welcoming, and creative environment. Whether it's building award-winning games or crafting engine technology that enables others to make visually stunning interactive experiences, we're always innovating. Being Epic means being a part of a team that continually strives to do right by our community and users. We're constantly innovating to raise the bar of engine and game development."

Size / stage / funding: Large private company (thousands of employees), backed by Tencent and Sony among others; not a runway-dependent startup. Well capitalized, though the games industry broadly has seen contraction and Epic itself has had past workforce reductions.

Locations / HQ: HQ in Cary, North Carolina. Offices include Seattle, Salt Lake City, Montreal, Vancouver, Yokohama and Guildford. Canadian engineering presence is real and substantial in Montreal and Vancouver.

Remote policy: Predominantly in-office. Public reporting and employee accounts describe corporate staff expected on campus roughly four days a week, with remote work having tightened from supervisor-approved arrangements to designated work-from-home days and a small annual allowance of remote half-days. Individual exceptions exist but are not the norm. Job listings are consistently tied to a named office city ("Montreal, Quebec, Canada", "Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada") or to "Multiple Locations", and Epic's pay ranges are published per office jurisdiction (New York City, California, Washington, British Columbia), which is itself evidence that roles are location-anchored rather than remote.

Remote-Canada eligibility: NO — effectively disqualifying for this candidate. Epic hires in Canada, but into its Vancouver and Montreal offices under an in-office expectation, not as remote-anywhere-in-Canada. There is no remote-Canada posting in the registry and no policy statement supporting one. Vancouver is not commutable from Kimberley, BC and relocation is off the table. Treat Canadian Epic roles as office-based unless an individual exception is negotiated up front.

Engineering & tech: Two clusters are relevant here and both are strong.
1. Developer Platform (Horde): "You'll work on Horde, our in-house platform that powers CI/CD, remote execution, build health, storage, and other services critical to developer iteration and workflows. This platform is key to delivering our internal game projects and supports numerous organizations outside of Epic." They want "distributed systems that handle scheduling, queueing, storage, and failure recovery at high volume", "experience building software behind CI/CD or developer infrastructure (job schedulers, remote execution, or artifact storage services)" and expert C#.
2. AI Platform — an unusually detailed and substantive agentic-AI infrastructure org: "an enterprise-grade stack of agentic AI systems that automates engineering workflows, accelerates developer productivity, and enables new kinds of collaboration across Epic's teams. We're not deploying off-the-shelf tools — we're building production systems from the ground up, across six interconnected platforms": Geppetto (multi-tenant platform for team AI agents in Slack), EMA/Epic Managed Agents (compute and workspace infrastructure for headless agent harness runs at scale), Hodor (MCP OAuth gateway, plugin runtime and governance layer for AI tool orchestration, cited at 1,500+ MAU and 700K+ tool executions), Multipass (agent identity, credential vault and authorization for non-human workloads), Vektor (org-wide memory plane with knowledge graph and hierarchical summarization) and Roost (cryptographically signed software distribution and a Claude Code plugin marketplace). The senior role covers "distributed systems, security, LLM integration, and developer tooling", NATS JetStream event-bus patterns, multi-tenant isolation, RBAC, circuit breakers, rate limiters, DLQ handling and idempotent replay, AES-256-GCM credential encryption, and on-call for Hodor. They explicitly ask for design documents "clear enough for async review, precise enough to serve as the implementation spec" and want 7+ years with a track record owning complex backend systems.
Cultural caveat in the AI Platform JD's own words: "This is foundational work that will define how AI is used inside Epic for the next decade. The scale is real, the problems are hard, and the team is small enough that every engineer makes a decisive architectural impact."

Compensation (see data/company-comp/): Epic publishes locale-specific base-pay ranges on every posting under transparency law, with the caveat that "each base pay range is relevant only for individuals who are residents of or will be expected to work within the specified locale", and notes most employees are also eligible for benefit plans and discretionary incentive programs. Notable / other: Epic is a games company, so on-call and live-service pressure exist, and the industry's crunch reputation is a legitimate thing to probe. Against that, the Developer Platform and AI Platform orgs are internal-facing infrastructure teams whose customers are Epic's own engineers, which is exactly the internal-consumer profile that suits well-scoped work.
