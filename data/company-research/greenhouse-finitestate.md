# Finite State — research
Provider/key: greenhouse:finitestate | company_type: product

What they do: Finite State sells a product-security and software-supply-chain risk platform aimed at manufacturers of connected and embedded devices. The core technical asset is deep **binary** analysis — they can decompose shipped firmware without source access — complemented by source-code scanning (SAST), binary software composition analysis (SCA), SBOM generation and management, and continuous vulnerability intelligence. Customers are device manufacturers and enterprises operating software embedded in critical infrastructure and regulated environments, who face mounting compliance pressure (SBOM mandates, FDA/EU device-security regimes) to prove what is inside the software they ship.

How they describe themselves: They position the platform as "AI-native," automating product security end to end — from deep binary analysis through prioritization and remediation across the software supply chain — and frame the value as complete visibility and control over the software supply chain, to "reduce risk, ensure compliance, and deliver secure products faster." The framing is compliance- and risk-driven rather than growth-hype; the domain is mission-critical and regulatory, which is the "boring, load-bearing" flavor rather than the consumer-cycle flavor.

Size / stage / funding: Private venture-backed startup, small. Leadership is Matt Wyckhouse (CEO, founder), with a recent executive build-out — Tim Quock as COO, Beth Linker as chief product officer, and Ann Miller appointed to scale product-security and software-supply-chain strategy. A wave of senior exec hires like that usually signals a company preparing to scale commercially. No headcount, current round, or profitability figure is published on their own pages.

Locations / HQ: US-based (Columbus, Ohio roots), operating distributed. Their pay bands are tiered by geography with "Tier 1 (San Francisco, New York, Seattle)" and "Tier 2 (All Other Locations)," which implies no single mandatory office.

Remote policy: Remote/distributed, with location-tiered compensation rather than a location requirement. Their JDs do not impose an office or hybrid expectation.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Verified yes, at the posting level.** Both relevant live reqs — " AI Software Engineer" and "Lead AI Software Engineer" — carry the location "United States or Canada." That is an explicit country-level allowance rather than an inference. What is *not* established from their own pages is the employment mechanism (Canadian entity vs. employer-of-record) or whether specific provinces are excluded; the postings say Canada without narrowing to a province. Worth confirming in a first conversation, but the constraint is met on the face of the req.

Engineering & tech: The technical substance is unusually deep for a security vendor — binary decomposition and firmware analysis are hard systems problems, not wrappers. The open engineering roles are all AI-flavored (AI Software Engineer, Lead AI Software Engineer, Senior Software Engineer AI/ML), which given the "AI-native platform" positioning means applying LLM/ML systems to triage, prioritize and remediate vulnerability findings at scale — an applied-AI-over-a-real-data-asset problem rather than prompt-wrapping.

Notable / other: Compensation is stated openly and is high. The Lead AI Software Engineer JD publishes two tiers — Tier 1 (SF/NY/Seattle) $230,000–$290,000 and Tier 2 (all other locations) $220,000–$270,000 — and the AI Software Engineer JD posts $227,000–$270,000, in each case base salary, with equity and benefits on top. Their own wording: "Our salary ranges are based on experience and geographic location."

Open relevant roles (sample): AI Software Engineer; Lead AI Software Engineer; Senior Software Engineer AI/ML.
