# Velora — research
Provider/key: greenhouse:velora | company_type: product

What they do: Velora is a **unified nonprofit operations software company** formed by merging three
existing products: **Aplos** (fund accounting), **Raisely** (fundraising) and **Keela** (donor
CRM). In their own words: *"We're excited to share that Aplos, Raisely, and Keela have come together
to form one unified company, Velora, with a shared mission: to help nonprofit organizations thrive.
While we continue to offer the products you know and love, we now operate as one team, dedicated to
making nonprofit work easier, more impactful, and more sustainable."* And: *"Together, our combined
expertise spans fundraising, donor management, financial tracking, and communications—offering a
powerful suite of tools designed to reduce complexity so nonprofits can focus on what matters most:
making a difference."* The current product line is Velora Fundraising (Raisely), Velora CRM (Keela),
Velora Fund Accounting (Aplos), with Velora Grant Management appearing in site navigation.

How they describe themselves: Company vision on their site: *"Your data connected. Your team ahead
of what matters. Your mission moving forward."* On working there: *"We're one company with 3
products all servicing customers in the impact space. Working at Velora means that you have the
opportunity to build one or across all our products. We have a combination of merged teams and also
teams dedicated to one specific product."* On pace and stability, stated plainly and together:
*"We move fast — we're an established, financially solid company, not an early-stage gamble — and we
need people who enjoy wearing many hats, adding value, and working shoulder-to-shoulder with the
wider engineering org. We hold one of the closest DevOps↔Engineering relationships you'll find
anywhere, and that's on purpose."*

Size / stage / funding: Established and self-described as **"financially solid... not an early-stage
gamble."** Serves **15,000+ nonprofits worldwide**; customers have raised over **$9 billion** through
its tools, supporting **10.5 million unique donors**. Post-merger consolidation phase (three
companies unifying into one platform), which is a real organizational-change context.

Locations / HQ: Distributed across Canada; Vancouver area is preferred but not required for the
platform role. Aplos brings a US footprint; Raisely and Keela have Australian and Canadian lineage.

Remote policy: **Remote-first**, stated as a benefit: *"🌴 Work remotely – We're a remote-first
company — live and work wherever you're happiest in Canada (excluding Quebec)."*

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Yes — explicitly, and British Columbia is named.** The DevOps/Platform
JD states: *"Location: Remote — Canada (Ontario, Manitoba, Alberta, British Columbia only);
Vancouver area preferred."* BC is in the permitted province list, and "Vancouver area preferred" is
a preference, not a requirement. Quebec is excluded. This is one of the only postings in the batch
that names British Columbia as an eligible remote province. Time zone: distributed Canadian team,
so Mountain Time is workable.

Engineering & tech: The DevOps/Platform role is genuine **internal developer platform** work, and
the JD is unusually clear that it means owning software, not just running it. Their framing:
*"This is a DevOps role with a platform engineering focus: building the internal developer platform
and paved roads that power how Velora engineering teams build, ship, and operate software (not the
product platform itself)."* And: *"At Velora, DevOps provides the tooling, infrastructure, and
processes that let engineers self-serve — safely and at speed. Many engineers have permission to
deploy services to production and own everything around them (configuration included) because we've
built the platform and guardrails that make that safe."* They **treat the internal platform as a
product**: *"we partner closely with engineers to understand pain points, requirements, and SDLC
needs, then we go build the solutions and operate them. That means DevOps owns core platform
evolution work (like upgrades and foundational improvements) so product engineering can keep
attention on customer and business problems."* The flagship artifact is an **internal orchestration
platform** — *"a real SaaS product that DevOps builds and operates, whose customers are Velora's
engineering teams... a full software stack: a frontend, a backend, a document database, and complex
distributed orchestration logic built on an event-driven architecture with Redpanda. Owning it
means owning the code, the architecture, and the roadmap — not just running it."* Cloud: **GCP**
(Raisely is the current top business priority), with exposure across all three brands as the
platform unifies. They want a *"full-spectrum engineer: someone who understands cloud deeply,
provisions and upgrades infrastructure, reasons about networking, memory, CPU, and virtualization —
and who can genuinely code, reason about distributed systems, and think through complex workflows.
An engineer who chooses to solve DevOps problems."*

Notable / other: Benefits are unusually strong and stated on the JD — 4 weeks paid leave plus 11
public holidays plus a **2-week end-of-year company shutdown**, generous paid sick leave, robust
medical/dental/vision/disability/life coverage, **4% retirement match**, and training/conference
budget. Recruiting contact is recruitment@joinvelora.com. Product websites: joinvelora.com,
keela.co, raisely.com, aplos.com.

Open relevant roles (sample): Senior DevOps Engineer/Platform Engineer; Software Engineer II -
Raisely.

Pay (stated on their own JDs): Senior DevOps/Platform Engineer — **$135,000–$165,000 CAD** base,
*"determined based on factors such as skills, education, experience, and geographic location."*
Software Engineer II - Raisely sits lower (see `data/company-comp.jsonl`, CAD 125–140k).
