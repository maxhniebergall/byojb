# Atomic Industries, Inc. — research
Provider/key: greenhouse:atomicindustriesinc | company_type: product

What they do: Atomic Industries builds an AI-driven platform for making manufacturing tooling — the molds and dies that most physical goods are formed in. In their words: "From cars and aerospace systems to medical devices and packaging, most physical goods begin life in a mold or are shaped by a manufacturing tool. Producing these tools has always been slow, manual, and dependent on scarce expertise, taking weeks or months. We're changing that." The platform "tackles the hardest problems in geometry, process planning, and fabrication, collapsing production timelines from months to days and soon, minutes." Critically, they are not a pure software vendor: "We don't just build software; we run a fully operational factory where our technology produces production-grade tooling every week, enabling tight feedback loops and rapid iteration."

How they describe themselves: Mission, verbatim: "to make manufacturing as agile and scalable as the digital world, and in doing so, rebuild the infrastructure of the physical economy." Positioning is explicitly industrial-reshoring: "At our Detroit headquarters, we combine the industrial DNA of America's manufacturing heartland with the speed, intelligence, and precision of Silicon Valley… we're restoring speed, flexibility, and capability to the American industrial base." They publish a four-point "How We Work" section rather than a values list, quoted in full:
- **Fast iteration:** "We deploy code daily and validate it in the factory every week"
- **Factory-first mindset:** "Engineers spend time on the shop floor to understand the real-world impact of their work"
- **Low ceremony, high ownership:** "We value well-written design docs, tested code, and real results — not meetings"
- **Collaborative culture:** "Geometry, AI, and robotics teams are tightly integrated and co-develop the product together"

Size / stage / funding: Private venture-backed startup, "Backed by top-tier investors" (unnamed in their own materials). No headcount, round or profitability disclosed anywhere first-party. Capital-intensive: they operate their own factory alongside the software business. Treat as early/growth stage and runway-dependent.

Locations / HQ: Detroit, Michigan — headquarters and the operational factory.

Engineering & tech: Platform Engineering team; the Senior Software Engineer role spans "CAD and mesh pipelines to simulation infrastructure and web APIs — to build the core backend and data services that power Atomic's platform." Named work: scalable backend systems and APIs in Python "and other languages"; services for "geometry processing, simulation orchestration, and real-time data handling"; schemas and pipelines over PostgreSQL, S3, Redis and RabbitMQ; owning CI/CD and deployment infra (GitHub Actions, HashiCorp stack); profiling "performance-critical workloads, including GPU-accelerated simulation tasks." Minimums: 7+ years, one systems language (Go, Rust or C++) plus Python, distributed systems / data pipelines / HPC experience, hybrid cloud + on-prem including "bare-metal GPU systems." Bonus: 3D geometry kernels (Parasolid, OpenCascade), CAD/CAM, simulation, robotics, hardware-in-the-loop. A separate Infrastructure Engineer (SRE) req covers "hybrid infrastructure that spans cloud, on-prem, and GPU compute environments" — Nomad/Kubernetes, Terraform, HashiCorp stack, Python/Bash/Go, GPU fleet management, observability, 5+ years. They explicitly note the senior role "is ideal for generalists who are excited to work across disciplines."
