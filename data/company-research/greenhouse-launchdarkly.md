# LaunchDarkly — research
Provider/key: greenhouse:launchdarkly | company_type: product

What they do: LaunchDarkly is a feature-management and experimentation platform for software teams — the category-defining feature-flag product. In their own words: "Modern software delivery was supposed to be the foundation for a thriving digital business but reality has proven otherwise. Slow, inefficient development cycles, costly outages, and fragmented customer experiences are preventing developers from building their best software. The LaunchDarkly platform helps developers innovate on new features faster while protecting them with a safety valve to instantly rewind when things go wrong." The platform "processes trillions of evaluations daily." Product lines visible in the open roles: core flag delivery, SDKs (including AI SDK work), Experimentation, Guardian (release safety/guarded rollouts), and Data Platform. Customers are engineering organisations — i.e. developers are the end users, which makes this a developer-tools/infrastructure company rather than a consumer or sales-led product.

How they describe themselves: They state the platform is built "to guide engineers to the next frontier of DevOps by: improving the velocity and stability of software releases, without the fear of end customer outages; delivering targeted experiences by easily personalizing features to customer cohorts; maximizing the business impact of every feature through the ability to experiment and optimize; coordinating the release and optimization of software to provide consistent experiences across mobile platforms and device types; improving the effectiveness and productivity of engineering teams." On culture, in their own words: "At LaunchDarkly, we believe in the power of teams. We're building a team that is humble, open, collaborative, respectful and kind." And on pay: "LaunchDarkly operates from a place of high trust and transparency; we are happy to state the pay range for our open roles to best align with your needs." Careers-page framing: "Take your career to new heights and change the way teams build and deliver the software that powers our world." They also publish an explicit recruiting-fraud warning (recruiters only contact from @launchdarkly.com or LinkedIn "Verified Recruiter" accounts; never ask for money) and an accommodations request form — both process-maturity signals.

Size / stage / funding: Late-stage private company, well established (last widely-reported valuation was in the multi-billions). Headcount and current funding stage are not disclosed on the careers page and were not verified in this pass. Not an early/runway-risk company.

Locations / HQ: Two offices listed on the careers page — **Oakland, CA** (1999 Harrison Street, Suite 1100) and **London, UK** (The Work Project, 1 Leadenhall St). All six engineering postings in the registry are "Remote - US."

Remote policy: Remote-heavy in practice — every open engineering role is "Remote - US." No formal remote/async policy statement was found on the careers page. Compensation is explicitly geographically zoned within the US (see below), which confirms a distributed US workforce with location-based pay.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **No — US-only, confirmed from their own pay language.** Every posting is "Remote - US," and the pay section defines the entire addressable geography as US zones: "Zone 1: San Francisco/Bay Area or NYC Metropolitan Area, Boston, Seattle; Zone 2: Irvine, LA, Monterey, Santa Barbara, Santa Rosa, Austin, Portland, Philadelphia, Chicago; Zone 3: **All other US locations**," footnoted "Within the United States, our geographic pay zones are defined by counties surrounding major metropolitan areas." A compensation framework whose catch-all tier is "all other US locations" leaves no band for a Canadian employee. The only non-US presence is a London office. This is a verified negative.

Engineering & tech: Go and Python for backend services and statistical computation; event-driven architectures, data pipelines and large-scale data processing; cloud (AWS, GCP) with infrastructure-as-code; warehouse-native/warehouse-agnostic systems across Snowflake, Databricks, Redshift and BigQuery. The Experimentation team is unusually deep on applied statistics: "hypothesis testing, sequential analysis, variance reduction (CUPED, Winsorization), power analysis," plus adaptive experimentation — "contextual bandit systems, Bayesian optimization, automated allocation beyond simple A/B tests," Thompson sampling, RL-based allocation. Engineers "own systems end to end, including on-call," with explicit ownership of "monitoring, observability, incident response, on-call. Robust telemetry and alerting." Levelling is public in the JDs ("Level 5" = Staff).

Notable / other: Pay transparency is genuinely strong — full three-zone ranges published on every posting, plus a statement of philosophy. Compensation includes RSUs, health, vision, dental and mental-health benefits "in addition to salary." The Staff Experimentation role is a rare blend of statistics depth and platform engineering. "Technical and Agentic Enablement Lead" and "SDK Software Engineer, AI" indicate real investment in agentic/AI developer tooling, though the Enablement Lead title suggests an internal-enablement/DevRel-adjacent role rather than pure platform building — worth checking scope before treating it as an infrastructure role.
