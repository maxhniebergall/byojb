# Miovision — research
Provider/key: ashby:miovision | company_type: product
What they do: Miovision builds traffic-management technology for cities — multimodal traffic data collection, traffic-signal planning and optimization, and intelligent signal control, "backed by the world's most advanced traffic AI". Products include Miovision One (consolidated software platform), Signal Optimizer, Opticom (emergency-vehicle and transit priority control), Scout Plus (portable traffic data collection) and Mateo (a GenAI agent for traffic engineers). Customers are municipalities and transportation agencies.
How they describe themselves (their own words): Vision/mission "to enable smart, fast, safe communities that simply flow, as we drive the Intelligent Mobility Revolution". Five stated values, quoted in full: "All in to win: We're driven by a winning mindset, approaching every challenge with intensity, clarity, and speed"; "One Miovision: We succeed as one team, uniting diverse talents, building on trust, and putting our shared mission before ego"; "Be better every day: We're committed to continuous growth, staying curious, building mastery, and embracing challenges as learning opportunities"; "Make it happen: We are proactive and results-driven, taking ownership, acting with urgency, and focusing on solutions that deliver real impact"; "Earn the customer: We are deeply customer-centric, focused on earning our customers' partnership every day". Corporate positioning is pointedly anti-lock-in: "Cities have been trapped in vendor-locked agreements for too long. Miovision puts cities back in control of their data."
Size / stage / funding: Private, founded by three University of Waterloo alumni; headcount not disclosed on the pages read. Established (well over a decade old) with a global office footprint, and it raised a large growth round earlier in its life — funding specifics not verified here. Benefits include RRSP/401K matching and a Variable Incentive Plan, indicating a mature HR function.
Locations / HQ: HQ Kitchener, Ontario, Canada. Additional offices: St. Paul MN, Portland OR, Pasadena and Irving TX, Munich, Dubai, Singapore, and Mérida Mexico.
Remote policy: Not stated as a formal company-wide policy on the site, but the live engineering req is posted as "Canada Remote", and benefits copy emphasizes "true work-life balance", "Mio-Days" and a flexible vacation policy. Distributed-friendly in practice for Canadian engineering.
Remote-Canada eligibility: YES — verified. The Senior Software Developer, API Platform req is listed with location "Canada Remote", and the company is Canadian-headquartered (Kitchener ON) with Canadian benefits (RRSP matching, provincial-style health coverage). Workable from Kimberley BC; note that the engineering centre of gravity is Eastern Time, so a Mountain-Time hire would be three hours behind the core team — not addressed in the posting.
Engineering & tech: Kotlin (primary) and Go, Kubernetes, Docker, AWS. Platform Engineering owns a stateless unified API gateway acting as "the highly performant front door for all Miovision applications and services", with usage tracking, billing hooks, tenant-aware access control and observability built in. Practices: Domain-Driven Design, API-first with OpenAPI, API governance (versioning, backward compatibility, API linting, contract testing), service catalogs and developer documentation. Edge auth via OIDC, OAuth 2.0 and Auth0. Explicit AI-agent infrastructure work: "Design machine-readable, agent-friendly API contracts that empower our AI chatbot (Mateo), MCP servers, and future AI agents to safely discover and invoke Miovision capabilities." Engineers are expected to use AI-assisted development tools (they name AMP).
Notable / other: Benefits: comprehensive health coverage from day one, 24/7 virtual healthcare, wellness programs, RRSP/401K matching, Variable Incentive Plan, "Mio-Days" and flexible vacation. Miovision discloses that it uses AI to assist in hiring but "do not use AI to make any final hiring or interview decisions". No salary figure is published on the req and no first-party pay-transparency page was found.
