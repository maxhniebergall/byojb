# Inferact — research
Provider/key: ashby:inferact | company_type: product

What they do: Inferact is the commercial company behind **vLLM**, the widely used open-source LLM inference and serving engine. In their own words: "Inferact's mission is to grow vLLM as the world's AI inference engine and accelerate AI progress by making inference cheaper and faster. Founded by the creators and core maintainers of vLLM, we sit at the intersection of models and hardware — a position that took years to build." The product surface is inference runtime, GPU kernels, distributed serving at scale, and cloud orchestration for running models in production.

How they describe themselves: Heavily autonomy-forward. "This role is designed for self-directed, autonomous individuals who can identify the highest-leverage problems and solve them end-to-end without constant guidance." "We're looking for engineers who thrive with autonomy. You should be able to take a vague problem statement and turn it into shipped code with minimal supervision. You communicate proactively, over-communicate context across time zones, and know when to ask for help versus when to push forward independently." Work is framed as spanning "the entire vLLM stack: from low-level GPU kernels to high-level distributed systems", with the example that "you might be optimizing CUDA kernels one week, designing distributed orchestration systems the next, and implementing new model architectures the week after".

Size / stage / funding: **Very early.** Launched publicly on 22 January 2026 with a **$150M seed round** at a reported ~$800M valuation, co-led by Andreessen Horowitz and Lightspeed, with Sequoia, Altimeter, Redpoint, ZhenFund, The House Fund, Striker, Laude Ventures, Databricks Ventures and the UC Berkeley Chancellor's Fund also participating. Founders are the vLLM maintainers — Simon Mo, Woosuk Kwon, Kaichao You, Roger Wang — with Ion Stoica (Databricks co-founder, director of UC Berkeley's Sky Computing Lab, where vLLM originated in 2023) among the founding team. Headcount is small; it is a months-old company, extremely well capitalized for its stage.

Locations / HQ: San Francisco headquarters; the engineering team is globally distributed.

Remote policy: **Fully remote, worldwide**, stated as such: "This is a globally remote opportunity… Location: Fully remote, worldwide. We're timezone-flexible but expect regular overlap with Pacific Time for critical syncs." Async-by-design: "You'll work asynchronously with our San Francisco headquarters while maintaining full ownership of critical infrastructure." Visas are sponsored case-by-case.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Yes on policy, unverified on mechanics.** "Fully remote, worldwide" plainly includes Canada, and Mountain Time is only one hour off Pacific — the best possible overlap situation. What is not stated anywhere is the employment vehicle for a non-US hire (employer-of-record, contractor, or a Canadian entity); at a six-month-old company this is a real open question and should be asked before investing in the process. Compensation is explicitly localized: "competitive compensations (salary + equity) compared to the local market conditions" — i.e. expect a Canada-adjusted, not SF, number.

Engineering & tech: The deepest technical surface in this batch. Named areas: **Inference Runtime** (LLM and diffusion serving inside vLLM core across diverse hardware), **Kernel Engineering** (CUDA, Triton, TileLang, Pallas; "hundreds of accelerator types"), **Performance & Scale** (distributed systems serving models across thousands of accelerators), and **Cloud Orchestration** (cluster management, deployment automation, production monitoring). Stack signals: Rust/Go/C++ for high-performance distributed systems, Python with PyTorch internals, Kubernetes and IaC at scale, transformer architectures and KV-cache memory management, quantization and compiler technologies, multiple accelerator platforms (NVIDIA, AMD, TPU, Intel). vLLM itself is open source, so the codebase and its engineering standards are fully public.

Notable / other: They prize public technical output — "written widely-shared technical blogs or impactful side projects in the ML infrastructure space" is a listed preferred qualification, as are contributions to vLLM or comparable OSS. The board title convention is "Member of Technical Staff", with no level distinctions published: Inference, Performance and Scale, Kernel Engineering, Cloud Orchestration, and Exceptional Generalist.

Open relevant roles (sample): Member of Technical Staff — Exceptional Generalist (Remote); Cloud Orchestration; Performance and Scale; Inference; Kernel Engineering.

Pay: No figure disclosed anywhere first-party — the JD says only "competitive compensations (salary + equity) compared to the local market conditions". No band recorded; a parametric estimate for a six-month-old company with no public comp data would be pure anchoring.
