# DNAstack — research
Provider/key: bamboohr:dnastack | company_type: product

What they do: DNAstack builds genomics and health-data software. Its flagship suite, **Omics AI**, lets institutions "connect, protect, and make AI-powered discoveries across federated networks of omics and health data — all in compliance with open industry standards." Products include **Publisher** (connect/share data over open standards), **Explorer** (federated data portal for discovery), **Workbench** (standards-compliant analysis), **Workflows**, and **Tertiary** (analysis powered by network intelligence). The core technical idea is federation — analysis across institutional datasets *without* centralizing sensitive patient data. Customers are research institutions, health systems, and national/consortium genomics programs.

How they describe themselves: Mission is to "save and improve lives by unlocking the collective power of the world's genomics and health data." Stated design principles are "privacy, security and interoperability by design," built on open standards from the **Global Alliance for Genomics & Health (GA4GH)** and compliant with HIPAA, GDPR, PIPEDA and SOC 2 Type 2. Public positioning emphasizes Canadian data sovereignty — recent coverage frames the company as leading "sovereign AI for genomics and health" with the goal of "making Canadians healthier and reducing strain on our healthcare system." Tone is scientific and standards-driven rather than hype-driven; the marketing language is about compliance, interoperability and research outcomes, not growth or disruption.

Size / stage / funding: Private, venture/grant-funded, small (roughly 30–80 people based on public profiles). Spun out of the University of Toronto; founded by Marc Fiume with James Vlasblom, Ryan Cook and Miro Cupak. Revenue is heavily tied to institutional and public-health contracts (Ontario genomics initiatives, national precision-health platforms), which gives it more stability than a typical VC startup but also grant-cycle dependence. Not disclosed as profitable.
