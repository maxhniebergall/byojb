# Enveritas — research
Provider/key: greenhouse:enveritas | company_type: product
What they do: Enveritas is a 501(c)(3) non-profit (Y Combinator-backed) that measures and reports on the social, economic and environmental conditions of smallholder coffee farmers. It runs a large-scale data-collection operation — identifying coffee farms in satellite imagery, coordinating survey work across country teams, detecting data anomalies in real time while surveyors are still in the field — and aggregates the results into reports for coffee roasters and other stakeholders. A major current workstream is EUDR (EU Deforestation Regulation) compliance: ingesting customer-submitted farm lists, validating and transforming them, and running them against a deforestation-detection model and geospatial datasets.
How they describe themselves: The Engineering & Data Group calls itself "a quirky, talented, and humble group of about thirty people with diverse backgrounds, ranging from journalism to academia to international industry", 36% of the group identifying as women and/or non-binary, several parents. They ask candidates to be "inspired by our mission to improve the lives of smallholder coffee farmers", to have "deep empathy for users of our tools", and — candidly — "a love for environments with uncertainty" because "operational and business needs can be ambiguous and change on a short time-scale". They emphasise fair and equitable hiring and publish their full interview process in advance ("four conversations that total about five hours", four to six weeks after the intro interview), and they explicitly value code legibility and maintainability "when in the hands of your teammates".
Size / stage / funding: ~150 people across more than two dozen countries; the Engineering & Data Group is about thirty. Non-profit, YC-backed — funded by grants/customer programs rather than a burn-rate equity story.
Locations / HQ: Fully distributed, no meaningful HQ. Country Operations teams in coffee-growing countries including Colombia, Costa Rica, Ethiopia and Indonesia.
Remote policy: Remote/global and fully distributed. Employment outside the US is through an EOR (Deel). Core hours are 10am–2pm Eastern, Monday–Friday, with team members choosing either an early start or a later stop.
