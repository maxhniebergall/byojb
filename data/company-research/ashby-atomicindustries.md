# Atomic Industries — research
Provider/key: ashby:atomicindustries | company_type: product

What they do: Atomic Industries builds an AI-driven platform for designing and producing manufacturing tooling — the molds and dies from which most physical goods originate. In their own words: "From cars and aerospace systems to medical devices and packaging, most physical goods begin life in a mold or are shaped by a manufacturing tool. Producing these tools has always been slow, manual, and dependent on scarce expertise, taking weeks or months. We're changing that." The platform "tackles the hardest problems in geometry, process planning, and fabrication, collapsing production timelines from months to days and soon, minutes." Critically, it is not pure software: "we run a fully operational factory where our technology produces production-grade tooling every week."

How they describe themselves: Mission framing is explicitly industrial-nationalist: "we're restoring speed, flexibility, and capability to the American industrial base. Our mission is to make manufacturing as agile and scalable as the digital world, and in doing so, rebuild the infrastructure of the physical economy." Their stated working principles, verbatim under "HOW WE WORK": "Fast iteration: We deploy code daily and validate it in the factory every week"; "Factory-first mindset: Engineers spend time on the shop floor to understand the real-world impact of their work"; "Low ceremony, high ownership: We value well-written design docs, tested code, and real results — not meetings"; "Collaborative culture: Geometry, AI, and robotics teams are tightly integrated and co-develop the product together."

Size / stage / funding: Venture-backed startup — "Backed by top-tier investors" is all they state; no round, valuation or headcount is disclosed and none was verified. The equity-heavy benefits ("Competitive salary and generous equity package") and the size of the ambition mark it as an early-to-mid-stage hardware-plus-software company. Capital intensity is high: they operate their own factory.

Locations / HQ: Detroit, Michigan headquarters, with an explicit Silicon Valley cultural graft: "At our Detroit headquarters, we combine the industrial DNA of America's manufacturing heartland with the speed, intelligence, and precision of Silicon Valley."

Remote policy: The posting is listed "Remote" in the registry with no qualifier, but the JD's own benefits contradict a straightforward distributed model: "Quarterly team travel to Detroit", "on-site prototype lab access", and the factory-first principle that "Engineers spend time on the shop floor". This role sits close to physical infrastructure.

Remote-Canada eligibility: UNVERIFIED and unlikely. The only immigration statement is "Visa support for TN, E-3, and O-1 candidates" — these are all US work visas, meaning the company's model is to bring people onto US soil and US payroll, not to employ them abroad. Combined with a Detroit factory, ITAR/CMMC compliance mentioned as a bonus qualification (ITAR in particular constrains who may access controlled technical data), and mandatory quarterly Detroit travel, a Canada-based remote arrangement should be treated as improbable until the employer says otherwise. Note the TN visa is the Canada/Mexico NAFTA-successor category — the plausible reading is that they expect a Canadian to relocate to Detroit, which is disqualifying.

Engineering & tech: The role (Infrastructure Engineer / SRE) is technically excellent on paper: "own the systems that power high-performance simulation, geometry processing, and factory automation... building and maintaining the hybrid infrastructure that spans cloud, on-prem, and GPU compute environments." Concretely: CI/CD with "GitHub Actions, Terraform, HashiCorp stack"; orchestrating "compute workloads across cloud and bare-metal GPU clusters"; secure fault-tolerant deployment and observability; internal developer tooling; performance tuning under high computational loads. Requirements: 5+ years DevOps/infra/SRE, container orchestration ("Nomad, Kubernetes"), IaC, Linux, "Python, Bash, Go", hybrid cloud + on-prem, distributed systems and observability. Bonus: ML/simulation/HPC workloads, "managing large GPU fleets or multi-node compute tasks", and security/compliance (ITAR, SOC2, CMMC).

Notable / other: Benefits stated: competitive salary and generous equity, full medical/dental/vision for employees and dependents, 401(k) (US retirement vehicle — another US-payroll signal), "PTO with a 15-day minimum", hardware stipend. Entity check: the JD's self-description matches the registry name and there is no evident collision with other "Atomic" companies (Atomic the VC studio, Atomic Financial) — this is the Detroit tooling manufacturer.
