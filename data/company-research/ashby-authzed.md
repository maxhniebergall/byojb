# AuthZed — research
Provider/key: ashby:authzed | company_type: product

What they do: Authorization infrastructure. AuthZed builds and commercializes **SpiceDB**, an open-source database for fine-grained permissions modelled on Google's Zanzibar paper — "the first open-source project to incorporate all three core design principles from Google's Zanzibar paper: strong consistency, global replication, and a relationship-based permissions model." The commercial products are a managed/dedicated SpiceDB service ("permissions-as-a-service"). Their current positioning extends this to AI agent authorization — deciding what an autonomous agent is allowed to do. In their own words, they build "the tools companies need to provide performant and scalable authorization for their applications, ensuring every request is authorized without compromising user experience." Customers span healthcare, finance and gaming; Canva is a named customer.

How they describe themselves: They summarize the culture in one sentence: **"Trust each other."** From that, "stems the agency for each AuthZedder personally to do their job well" — everyone should have "the capability, freedom, and confidence to bring about changes to the business and product," and "organizational processes for remote teams exist to clearly define goals but not restrict how progress is made." On being remote they are emphatic: dedicated "to not being just remote-friendly, but remote-first," and the culture is **"written-first, where the ability to explain complex technical issues in plain English is key."** Engineering values "the ability to spot a problem, scope a solution, and drive it to completion without waiting for a perfect spec." The SRE JD adds: "AuthZed is a fully remote company with employees across the US, Canada, and Europe. We're a hardworking and close-knit group with a software-driven culture (yep, even our GTM team understands and loves this technology)! We bring integrity to all our interactions, fostering confidence in decision making - trusting and respecting each voice on our team, every day."

Size / stage / funding: Founded 2020 in New York by Jake Moshenko (CEO), Joseph Schorr and Jimmy Zelinskie — the team behind Quay.io, later acquired by CoreOS/Red Hat. **Series A of $12M (June 2024) led by General Catalyst**, with returning investors Work-Bench and Amplify Partners; ~$15.8M raised in total. Reported 6x ARR growth in 2023. Small — on the order of 25–40 people. Early-stage and venture-dependent.

Locations / HQ: Nominally New York, but there is no office model — employees are distributed across the **US, Canada and Europe**.

Remote policy: Fully remote and explicitly remote-first, not remote-friendly. Written/async-first documentation culture. The SRE JD offers "a full remote and flexible schedule **to accommodate different timezones**." They run regular company off-sites, which they describe as "vital to the life of the remote company," so expect periodic travel.

Remote-Canada eligibility: **Verified yes.** All three postings on record are scoped "**United States, Canada**", and the company states directly in its own JD that it has "employees across the US, Canada, and Europe." Combined with a flexible schedule stated to accommodate different timezones, Mountain Time should be workable — though a US/Europe centre of gravity means some early-morning overlap.

Engineering & tech: Go is the primary language (SpiceDB is Go); the problem space is distributed databases — consistency models, caching, global replication, and the Zanzibar consistency protocol. Roles on record are split between a distributed-databases team, a control-plane team, and SRE. Development happens in the open on GitHub, and they publish a technical blog including a detailed writeup of their engineering interview process. This is genuinely specialized, deep systems work.

Notable / other: Founders have a strong prior track record (Quay container registry → CoreOS → Red Hat), which materially de-risks an early-stage bet. SpiceDB has real open-source mindshare as the reference Zanzibar implementation. Compensation on their live JDs is transparent and equity is offered. Benefits language is modest: "Competitive salary based on experience."

Open relevant roles (sample): Sr. Site Reliability Engineer (US/Canada); Sr. Software Engineer - Distributed Databases (US/Canada); Sr. Software Engineer: Control Plane (US/Canada).
