# Dispatch — research
Provider/key: ashby:dispatch | company_type: product

What they do: Dispatch describes itself as "the first wealth tech data orchestration company". It syncs client data across the 7–10 software tools a financial advisor uses to run their business, so an advisor gets "the efficiency of a fully-integrated software suite with the choice of tools that best fit their business". The pitch frames the US financial-advisory industry as an "$80T market" that has seen little technological innovation relative to self-service players (Robinhood, Betterment, Wealthfront, SoFi), and identifies the pain as manual collection/transcription of client data across systems, plus access control, audit logs and compliance in larger organizations.

How they describe themselves: The "Why Dispatch?" section is their own list, verbatim in substance: "We're a highly experienced team with a track record of successfully building together. We're well-versed in building for the best tech companies and asset managers"; "We're a well-funded company backed by top-tier investors"; "We're laser-focused on customers and believe that a world-class user experience is critical for product and business success"; "We move fast, iterate quickly, and have a high bar for what 'excellent' looks like." They describe the team as "an experienced team of start-up operators with deep roots in both tech and advisory". The JD's requirements repeat the same posture: "Bias toward action and ownership", "Thrive in fast-paced environments with evolving priorities and high autonomy", and a stated expectation of AI-tool fluency ("you leverage AI in your workflow and can help level up the team's capabilities"). A nice-to-have is "Experience at seed/early-stage startups where you wore multiple hats and moved quickly."

Requirements: 4–6 years backend, API design, data modeling, concurrent systems, performance optimization, relational databases, distributed systems concepts. Nice-to-haves: ETL pipelines, third-party APIs at scale, fintech/B2B SaaS, event-driven architectures, message queues, data synchronization patterns, open-source contributions.
